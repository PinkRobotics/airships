Pink Robotics — Airships · The build, staged

No cell floats.
The ship does.

For a season the question here was how to make one vacuum cell lighter than the air it displaces. The verified answer is that it never will be — and never needed to be. A cell is a wall unit. A ship is a machine built from them: a deep lattice shell carrying the sky's whole squeeze, a band of cells killing the pressure at the skin, and a core of engineered nothing doing the lifting. This page is the new spine of the site — seven levels, from sintered powder and rolled fibre to a working ship on a fire line.

Level 1 · The catalog

Everything begins as powder, fibre, or film: titanium sintered into split clamshells, carbon rolled onto mandrels, membrane pressed into its dome before it ever meets a frame. These are the parts — each one browsable below, each carrying its own honesty chip. Two panes so any two parts can be compared; the arrows walk each catalog.

Level 2 · The cell

One Kelvin cell: the first buildable block, deliberately not lighter than air. It assembles from the catalog's parts, wears its skin, takes its vacuum, and proves every joint and film load the ship will reuse a hundred thousand times. The explorer below is that cell today — assemble it from a pile, peel its skin, pump it down, weigh it against the sky.

Where the numbers come from

Cell figures on this page are read live from cell/model.js — the same committed module the explorer runs, billed from the measured saw table and the weighed joints. Ship figures are the verified scoping analysis and wear a scoping chip until the ship module lands under the same gates the cell enjoys. Flags are never hidden; a part with an unverified number says so on its card.
