One number decides the whole project. A vacuum shell heavier than the wall has no net lift at any size, ever. Lighter, and the hull can simply be grown until lift covers the ledger. Every level below this one exists to get under it.

The hull is just the array, continued until it is a vehicle. Machinery sits in ambient bays bolted to structure the cells pull up on. Nothing operates in vacuum.

Structure inside structure is what closes the gap. Each level of hierarchy improves the strength-to-density exponent — (n+2)/(n+1), tending to linear (Lakes, Nature 361, 1993) — and it converges fast:

Level 2 is the design target. Beyond it the exponent gains shrink while every added level still costs joints, tolerance and inspection. The final ships are generatively designed to these specs — load-graded, built and maintained by machine — but the rule they follow is this table.

Sealed at every scale. Sub-cells inside cells, cells inside sections — each level evacuated and sealed, so no partition ever faces more than its own level's differential and a breach is bounded by the smallest enclosure containing it. A section is a cell of cells.

The pumped plenum is that doctrine made active. A soft shell across the whole ship holds as much vacuum as its pumps can — lossy, never sealed — so no interior cell works against a full atmosphere. It buys no lattice mass, because statics still delivers one atmosphere to the array through the shell's mounts. What it buys is margin: a cell proven to × of its operating load instead of scraping past it.

The ambient bay holds pumps, tanks and plant at one atmosphere inside the ship — ordinary machinery, ordinary maintenance, surrounded by the cells that carry it.

Open on this level: whether interior films are sized to contain a breach (0.347 kg/m³) or only to seal (nearly free, but a breach cascades) — the report calls that the sharpest unresolved trade in the design, and sealing at every scale is the lever that shrinks it.

Why cells at all. A monolithic vacuum balloon dies by shell buckling — a scale-free result no material escapes. Fill the volume with sealed cells and the atmosphere is held only at the boundary: a cellular solid in hydrostatic compression, which is a well-posed engineering problem. What cells buy is the removal of the shape penalty and the containment of a breach: the atmosphere is held at the boundary, and a puncture floods one cell rather than the ship.

Click a cell to flood it. A breach is a local load. The flooded cell's neighbours see one atmosphere across their shared walls — a factor-L concentration costing L^⅔ in local density right there, not a redesign of the ship:

case×L^⅔kg/m³ local margin

Margins at the level-2 target ( kg/m³ with the envelope film). The demonstrable single-level design is under the wall before any breach — these margins are what the ladder buys.

The partition shape is the Kelvin cell — the truncated octahedron, BCC's Voronoi cell, the interlocking near-sphere. It carries % less film area than cubes at equal volume, and the analysis charges the cube figure regardless:

The skin wears its lattice — the brightened cell shows it. At half the cell pitch, the sub-lattice's nodes land exactly in the Kelvin surface: corner nodes in the hexagons, axis nodes in the squares (Kelvin is BCC's Voronoi cell, and the half-pitch octet grid contains the BCC points). The membrane is bonded at that regular grid of boundary nodes and its films bulge between them — which is why the partition shape and the lattice inside it are the same geometry, not two that have to be made to agree.

The shape. A truncated octahedron fills space with the least wall per unit volume — eight hexagons, six squares, thirty-six edges. Near-spherical, so the buckling radius is the cell's own; space-filling, so cells seat face to face and the array becomes the airframe rather than cargo inside one.

The interior and the boundary do different jobs. The interior is pure compression — one mm cut, 144 times. The boundary is not: the film does not push on the lattice, it pulls the edge frame sideways, and at a dihedral the two panels' tensions add instead of cancelling. An unbraced rim of this section carries only atm — less than half what the cell holds — so every face is braced in its own plane: the squares by vertex ties, the hexagons by six radial spokes each.

Bracing pays twice. Bending falls as the cube of the bracing pitch, so spokes buy strength cheaply — and they buy back volume. Every panel dimples inward and that dent is open to the sky, so an unbraced cell forfeits % of its own displacement. Spoked, %. On anything meant to float, that is a fifth of the lift.

Every member is bought; every joint is printed solid. Tube at × Euler margin even pinned, and joints in Bambu PAHT-CF (chopped-carbon nylon, kg/m³) weighed from their own geometry rather than budgeted. Solid because these parts sit inside the vacuum, where sparse infill is a virtual leak. The parts button takes the tubes away.

This cell does not float, and printing is not what decides it. To float it would have to weigh under g — the air it displaces. It weighs kg, and that mass is kg of bought tube against kg of printed joints. The tube is five sixths of it. Even with joints of zero mass this cell is an order of magnitude over.

What decides it is size, and then the tube's grade. The budget is mass per cubic metre enclosed, so volume grows as the cube of the span while the frame inside it grows far more slowly — a bigger cell of the same architecture is lighter per litre, not heavier. Two things follow. A hexagon face at this span contains no lattice point of its own, which is why it needs the rim, spokes and ties you can see; one size up the face is full of them and that apparatus goes away. And in M60J-class tube rather than the T700-class this cell is cut from, the same geometry reaches kg/m³ — buoyant in sea-level air as a single cell, where T700 needs levels of frame inside frame to get there.

This is the bench article, and the order it is built in is the

test: baked out under vacuum, the barrier bonded hot while the cell is already in the chamber, and the seal closed as the last operation before it cools. The chamber does the evacuating — there is no valve and no pump aboard, so nothing is pumped down afterwards. The crush comes last and it is not a step anyone performs: the cell comes out of the chamber and one atmosphere stands on it.

The same geometry printed end to end in nylon — the variant that proves the printer chain rather than the physics — comes to kg against g of displaced air.

These are the only designed parts in the airship. Everything else is bought: stock tube, stock film. Every joint grows from one signed distance field — spigots blended into a core, bores and an annular seating slot cut afterwards, the flat mating lands truncated last so no blend can round one off. Change a diameter and all regrow.

Capture is internal, and it grips three ways at once. The pipe slides over a hollow spigot, a collar closes outside it, and both bottom on a shoulder. The bore is the mandrel-formed face of the tube — the roundest surface it has — which is why the spigot indexes there and the compliance lives outside. MPa on the glue line, and the spigot's own section carries MPa.

The nozzle no longer sets the size of the article — it sets the size of these. When the members were printed too, a mm bead forced every dimension above it: wall, strut, cell. Buying the tube broke that chain. What the bead still governs is the joinery — perimeters of it make the mm wall these parts are built from. They print solid, and not for strength: they live inside the vacuum, where sparse infill is a virtual leak.

Z is the governing direction, so every figure here uses the weak one. A printed part is far weaker between its layers than along them — MPa interlayer against MPa in plane. A pressure vessel is loaded in every direction at once, so this page quotes the interlayer number throughout, never the datasheet's lead line.

What separates the families is not the arm count, it is the angle. An annular slot is a tube of outer radius mm around each arm, and two slots at angle θ intersect until the slot starts beyond that radius over the sine of the half-angle. Every family but the centre has a ° pair somewhere, so their slots begin mm out; the cell centre's tightest pair opens to °, so its slots begin at mm. That is where a mm member stops being a member and starts being a cut. Nothing in a render shows it. It shows up on the bench as a pipe that fouls on the remains of its neighbour's slot and never reaches its seat.

Two more things these meshes are not yet honest about, stated rather than smoothed over. The nominal mm shoulder is not reachable: the printed collar ends mm from the node centre while the slot-clearance rule starts the seat at – mm, so the pipe lands on the blend and this page does not publish a bearing stress. And the crush ribs are subtracted by the slot over the whole engagement — at mm they are of the mm voxel these meshes were extracted at, and the fit clearance is of one. The shipped STLs are geometry of record for form, not for fit.

× the orthotropy toll

local buckling rides √(Eaxial·Ehoop), not the datasheet number

Roll-wrapped, not pultruded, and this is not a preference. A composite tube wall is not one material, it is fibres pointing somewhere. Local buckling of an orthotropic tube rides √(Ex·Eθ), so the governing modulus is Ex^¾·Eθ^¼ and the best [0/90] split — three quarters axial, one quarter hoop — delivers of the fibre's headline stiffness. That factor has been in this model since the day the orthotropic penalty was written. A pultrusion is all-axial fibre: buying one would contradict the physics the article is priced by, and it would split at the sockets, where a spigot presses outward on hoop fibre a pultrusion does not have. Specify a [0/±45/90] wall, roll-wrapped over a mandrel — the bore is then the mandrel-formed surface, which is why the spigot indexes on the bore and the compliance lives outside it. The model prices the tube as .

What the tube is asked for. N per strut, × on Euler even pinned, × socketed, × on stress. And the published Euler figure is the conservative one twice over: the model takes the whole centre-to-centre length as the free length, but the member is held at its shoulders, over the mm cut. On that length it is × — a factor of better, and not claimed anywhere the model is used.

The wall thickness is set by the co-critical rule: thin enough that local buckling and Euler arrive together, thick enough that somebody can wrap it. That proportion fixes the wall as a constant fraction of strut length — which is why the printer's thinnest reliable wall set the cell size before a single member was purchased, and the whole chain still runs backwards from the nozzle:

nozzlewallstrut cellencloses

co-critical here is R/t · the flight reference optimises to R/t · the tube we buy is R/t

At floating densities struts are so slender that Euler buckling governs, not strength — solid rods give φ^ scaling and nothing floats. Hollow tubes restore φ^, if they are proportioned so that the strut's Euler load and the wall's local buckling arrive together. A catalogue section is not that proportion, and this page will not pretend otherwise: at R/t the bought tube is far inside co-critical, so local buckling is nowhere near it and the wall is carrying mass it does not need. That is the right trade at demonstrator scale — a stock section beats an optimal one you cannot order — and it is a trade the flight article cannot make. The proportion, not the topology, is what the mass turns on. And this wall is where the next rung lives: make it a lattice — two thin skins on a micro-truss core — and the exponent moves from to , the difference between and kg/m³ (× over the wall).

Sourcing note, and it is prose: Rock West Composites sell roll-wrapped tube in these sections, including UHM grades. No catalogue number is committed here and no vendor figure enters the model — the sizes are the model's, and the only claim is that they are ordinary stock rather than a special order.

The material is bought by the metre. Dyneema composite fabric — a UHMWPE fibre grid laminated between films, the same stock as ultralight tents.

A panel does not push on the lattice — it pulls on the frame. Each one bulges inward until its membrane tension balances the atmosphere, then stands at that angle to the face plane. That is the load the rim and the spokes were sized against, and the reason the boundary needed a heavier tube than the interior.

Film mass scales with the span it bulges across, which is the whole reason the spokes exist: cut a hexagon into six triangles and each panel spans a fraction of what the bare face did. It is also why no cell size makes the film term go away — the areal mass falls exactly as fast as area-per-volume does.

The fabric is sized for strength, not for holding vacuum. Those are different requirements. Holding a vacuum for years is a different requirement: the film is a membrane, not a barrier, and a real barrier laminate of the measured class is several times heavier. Two things remain open: how the barrier is applied without cracking on first pump-down, and how the seams close. See the panels below.

The dihedral edge

joints · flat lands

The mating land

Where the film is supposed to be bonded, and where cells seat face to face. of the printed joints carry one or more — on a square centre, on a hub, at a corner where one square and two hexagons meet — flat faces in all, truncated last in the SDF so no blend can round one back off.

A hub collects N of a hexagon's load and hands it to three props at N each. The printed pad the design once carried is gone — the manifest ships pad_r = — and the arithmetic is why: a disc only picks up what the film hands it around its own perimeter, so at mm across it could collect N, and it would have had to be mm across — wider than the hexagon — to take the hub's share. The spokes carry it. A pad was only ever local protection against a point support puncturing a film microns thick.

And nothing has been chosen to go on these lands. No adhesive, no bond-line width, no shear allowable, no peel case exists anywhere in this repository. The lands are designed; the bond is not. That is the honest and more interesting claim, and it is E6's job.

The bulge tax. Every panel dimples inward — mm on a spoked hexagon, on a square, on a bare hexagon — and that dimple is open to the sky, so the article displaces less air than its outline claims. Braced, the bulges swallow litres, % of the cell's litres. Unbraced, %. On the bench that is cosmetic. On a floater, where mass and displaced air are equal by construction, it is a fifth of the lift — a better argument for bracing than film mass ever was.

tonnes-force, standing on the fabric

N over m² of film and kg of tube

What is unresolved, and it is three things. E6 is the coupon that settles them, and none of it is softened here.

It is a membrane, not a barrier. The g/m² above is sized for strength. A real barrier laminate of the class this project has measured is several times heavier, and wrapped around a cell this size that is lift-negative on its own skin — deliberately, because the demonstrator is not meant to be flight representative. Only a deposited stack scales to every cell size, and nobody has a measured number for one on printed PAHT-CF. That is experiment E1, and it must be measured rather than argued. The arithmetic behind that comparison lives in research/notes/barrier-and-permeation.md, marked as arithmetic and under no checker, so this page does not put a figure on it.

The bulge is assumed, not achieved. Every number above takes the panel's dimple as of its half-span, and that needs a membrane strain several times what this fibre delivers at the working stress above. Either the film is pre-formed to its loaded dome before it is bonded, or the reachable bulge is far shallower and the film is several times heavier — which sinks it. The same strain would craze any inorganic barrier coating on first pump-down. That arithmetic is generated, in research/analysis/vacuum-cell.json under designPoint, but it is not exposed through the model this page reads, so the problem is stated and the figure is not.

The bond is undesigned, as the land card says. And Dyneema creeps under permanent load, so the spec is a heat-treated rigging grade or a Vectran laminate instead.

This is deferred on purpose. The structure is sized, gated and buildable; the skin is a fabric problem behind it, and pretending otherwise would be the one place this page lied.
