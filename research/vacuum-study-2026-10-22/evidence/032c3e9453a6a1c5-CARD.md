# MAINLINE-064: a supported knockdown γ for P2's sandwich sphere

Unit vacuum-mainline064-gamma-sources-1020. Estimate. Every inherited number is QUESTIONABLE. The density targets are PROJECTION. Nothing here is a certification, and there is no qualified hull safety factor.

## The published record

Sandwich external-pressure tests come first. NASA CR-1457 (Sullins, Smith and Spier, December 1969, NTRS 19700004831) is the handbook this model already uses. Section 6.2 says useful sandwich-dome data were scarce. The only set it kept is North American Rockwell's 55-inch S-II common-bulkhead models, Gonzalez and Patton, report SDL 468, May 1964. Figure 6.2-3 plots two hemisphere points and six approximately ellipsoidal points. Three further specimens were dropped because the membrane stress was too far into plasticity for a reduction factor, and one was dropped for a faulty edge. Two retained domes also carried a thermal gradient, inner face about +280 F and outer face about +10 F, which the reduction ignored. The text does not tabulate each point's knockdown, R/t, Vc or imperfection amplitude. The design rule taken from that figure is γ = 0.20 for hemispheres when Vc is below 2, and γ = 1 when Vc is at or above 2, where the mode is shear crimping and imperfections are said not to matter. Ellipsoids and torispheres are given 0.35 below Vc 2. The same section says a continuous knockdown between those branches was beyond the handbook, and that the rule is a rough guideline whose final designs must be substantiated by test. Yao's 1962 sandwich-sphere solution is the theory being knocked down, not a test. The flight S-II bulkhead, from the public fabrication accounts, is 2014-T6 aluminum faces on a glass-fabric honeycomb, a bonded dome, not a closed sphere.

Monocoque spheres do not raise that floor. Reviews of NASA SP-8032 (1969) state a deep-shell lower bound near 0.14. That is an isotropic thin shell, not a honeycomb sandwich. Hutchinson and Thompson (2018) report the Lee hemisphere series: near-perfect shells at R/t about 108 reached about 0.7 to 0.8 of classical, and a dimple deeper than about one thickness fell to about 0.2. Those are precision monocoque hemispheres. Sim, Park and others (2024) report a foam-core hemisphere calculation, not a test, at 0.20 for a dimple and 0.22 for a flat imperfection, set beside SP-8032 at 0.18 and CR-1457 at 0.20. NASA's later sandwich cylinder tests are axial compression of composite cylinders. CR-1457 already refused to treat cylinder data as the dome knockdown.

The two private papers are cited by DOI only. 10.2514/3.5568 and 10.2514/3.1932 are not external-pressure tests of a sphere or a sandwich dome. They supply no γ.

## Each source, judged for a bonded beryllium face on CR-III 5056 honeycomb at Vc 0.9 to 1.1

CR-1457's hemisphere value 0.20 is admissible with a named limit. The limit is that it is a 1969 design floor from two aluminum/glass-honeycomb hemisphere points on a 55-inch bulkhead model, not a measurement on this face, this core, this ribbon direction, this cure, or a closed 20 to 30 m shell, and not a function of imperfection amplitude. The specimens' own Vc is not in the text. The rule's Vc < 2 branch does cover 0.9 to 1.1.

CR-1457's γ = 1 branch is not admissible on this load path. It applies at Vc at or above 2. The optima below stay under 2.

The ellipsoid value 0.35 is not admissible for this sphere. Different contour.

Yao 1962 is not admissible as a measured knockdown. It is analysis.

The favorable ramp, 0.2 + 0.4 times the lesser of Vc and 2, is not admissible. CR-1457 says that continuous transition was not written. 063's favorable points sit at Vc 0.985 to 1.011, where the ramp is 0.594 to 0.604, about three times 0.20.

SP-8032's monocoque floor is admissible only as a warning that imperfect isotropic spheres fall to about 0.14. It does not support a sandwich γ above 0.20.

The Lee amplitude curve is not admissible for transfer. Product form, R/t and the seeded dimple are not this sandwich. It is the only published amplitude function found, and it does not apply.

The 2024 foam-core calculation and the sandwich-cylinder tests are not admissible as this γ.

No published test supports a γ above 0.20 for this construction. The value P2 can carry today is 0.20, not a range.

## The answer

A published γ does not close any cell. Holding γ at 0.20 makes every concept and TRL 7 payload negative. The apparent near-closure was the ramp.

The improvement, if the test returns 0.20, is a lighter face at beryllium's modulus. At R 30 in ISA that is 1238 kg/m3 when Q stays 0.82, or 889 kg/m3 when Q is 0.33. Specific modulus has to rise by the same factor, from about 159 MPa per kg/m3 to about 237, or to about 330. That is a different face.

T7 still has to be a closed sandwich shell in this product form, at Vc 0.9 to 1.1, under external pressure, in the elastic range, with the imperfection amplitude, the core ribbon direction and the cure state recorded. It has to show whether γ can stand above 0.20. Until that measurement, P2 carries 0.20.
