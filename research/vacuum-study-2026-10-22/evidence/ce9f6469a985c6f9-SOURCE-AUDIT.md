## Corrections and distinctions

The classical .09/.65–.70 optical range appears in the [patent](https://patents.justia.com/patent/11391493); that is the relevant alternative citation, rather than attributing .09/.65 to NASA 2015 slide19. Its emittance statistic and transfer to the specified deposited stack on Be still need evidence. NASA 2018 supports a coating class, not a measured recipe/substrate pair.

The SolarWhite sheet uses a CoBlast primer and gives a nominal product mass. It reports VUV exposure from α .24 to .28 at 7444 equivalent sun hours with ε .83, and a separate 100-cycle ±180°C case at .18/.84 before and after. These specimens must not be silently combined with the typical .18/.89 pair. Its outgassing values are not water-sorption data. The website lists Be compatibility, but the sheet's specific optical table is for aluminum; neither supplies the proposed installed Be mass and durability qualification.

The 016 0.1–1 mm band and 019 planning scenarios support carrying both scenarios. They establish neither a measured retention distribution nor 1 mm as an upper bound. The addendum correctly preserves exterior wet-service water after each internal conditioning route, and separately includes balanced cure compression in the global and crimp demands. Numerical reproduction of those alternatives does not validate their physical residual field.

No current check changes QUESTIONABLE source transfer, PROJECTION processing/operation, or null qualified installed-hull safety factors. Source omissions become corrections or open evidence requests; they do not stop the numerical card.
