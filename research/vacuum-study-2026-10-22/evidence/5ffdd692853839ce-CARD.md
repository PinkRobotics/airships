# MAINLINE-071 graphite conditions

Unit vacuum-mainline071-graphite-conditions-1020. QUESTIONABLE. PROJECTION. No qualified hull safety factor. One simjob. No finite-element run.

## The gamma step above Vc 2

CR-1457 section 6.2 sets the hemisphere knockdown at 0.20 except where shear crimping occurs, and sets it at 1 when Vc is above 2. The same section tells the designer to use the minimum core shear modulus when crimping is critical. The cylinder discussion that supplies the Vc relation says the region above 2 is shear crimping, that initial imperfections do not control it, and that the knockdown there is unity. No separate knockdown is written on the crimping stress. A continuous transition was outside the handbook.

070's gamma-step optima sit at Vc about 3.05 to 3.13, and core shear crimping co-binds. Version 7's step to 1 matches that rule. It is admissible with a named limit. The limit is that the rule is the 1969 weak-core statement, not a test of this graphite film on CR-III, not a closed 20 to 30 m sphere, and the model's core modulus is not shown to be the minimum of the layup.

DOI 10.2514/3.5568 is axial compression of imperfect cylinders. DOI 10.2514/3.1932 is a numerical bending method for shells of revolution. Neither governs a sandwich sphere above Vc 2, and neither supplies a crimping knockdown.

## The thermal interface

The film paper does not state an expansion in the text opened for 068. The signed value used is the HOPG catalogue magnitude, -1.0e-6 per K. That is a different product. The installed 12.3e-6 is the model's aluminum-minus-beryllium mismatch, so the coefficient becomes 24.6e-6. Face self-stress keeps the 1.0e-6 magnitude from 070. The negative sign is not credited there.

Every gamma-step case is then infeasible. At 070's geometry the thermal margin is 0.536 and the payload mass is unchanged. Face density does not enter the interface stress, so no density factor restores a shell. The service temperature swing does. The cure temperature does not: it is not in that stress.

A core expansion of 12.2e-6 per K would just restore a feasible shell, at this core's stiffness. That is the beryllium face's expansion, not an aluminum core. The 25 psi bond screen has to rise by 1.866, to 0.322 MPa. The core's minimum-average W plate shear, as the joining card states it, is 0.172 MPa, and the typical W value is 0.241 MPa. The bond factor is above the core.

## The scarf

The model uses 10 MPa. The joining card calls that an owner-selected average-shear screen, not a measured scarf allowable. The ratio is 30.34. At the thermal boundary the scarf margin is about 2.89. Doubling the screen to 20 MPa does not change the payload. Scarf is not the release.

## The answer

On this aluminum core the graphite route has no feasible shell. Gamma 1 is the handbook's crimping rule, with the named limit above. The improvement the interface still needs is a core expansion near 12.2e-6 per K, not a stronger bond and not a cure-temperature change. At the largest mismatch that is just feasible, R 20 optimistic still needs a face-density factor of 3.467 at concept and 3.137 at TRL 7, to 649 and 717 kg/m3, and that core thickness is the search floor. Those factors are worse than 070's 1.248 and 1.126, which left the beryllium mismatch in place.
