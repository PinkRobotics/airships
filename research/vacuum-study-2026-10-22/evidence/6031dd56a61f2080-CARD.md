# MAINLINE-010 NFI-GEOM: the geometry levers at fixed material — ESTIMATE_PENDING_CHECKER

Unit vacuum-mainline010-nfi-geom-1019. Everything QUESTIONABLE. Material stays 6061-T6, mass rules stay MAINLINE-002/003: faces and core on sphere area, joints 5% of sandwich, systems 100 kg. Thicknesses stay at each baseline's values unless a lever says otherwise. Crush stress is the MAINLINE-005b p/2 model. Baselines at 10 m, 500 m ISA: P2-A buckling SF 0.231, yield SF 0.435, margin 0; P2-E buckling SF 0.234, yield SF 0.389, margin 0. The ISA troposphere used here reproduces the pinned 500 m pressure within 0.1 Pa.

At fixed thickness, buckling pressure falls as 1/R^2 and yield as 1/R, while structural mass grows as R^2 against lift R^3. Almost all of that is real geometry. The fixed 100 kg systems allowance spread is worth only 0.018-0.020 of margin at 20-40 m, and it runs the other way at 5 m, where the unscaled 100 kg costs 0.143 of margin. Joints, being a fraction, spread nothing.

## Lever 2, altitude. SF rises, lift falls faster

| vs | altitude (m) | margin | buckling SF | yield SF | crush (Pa) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P2-E | 0 | 0.047 | 0.220 | 0.366 | 50663 |
| P2-E | 500 | 0 | 0.234 | 0.389 | 47730 |
| P2-E | 2000 | -0.160 | 0.281 | 0.467 | 39748 |
| P2-E | 5000 | -0.586 | 0.413 | 0.687 | 27010 |
| P2-A | 5000 | -0.586 | 0.408 | 0.768 | 27010 |

Pressure and lift density fall together up the ISA column. The limits do not move at fixed geometry, so SF climbs as 1/p, a factor 1.77 by 5000 m. The mass does not fall, so margin goes negative from 2000 m. Net: altitude buys SF with lift the design does not have. It is a trade, not a gain.

## Lever 3, partial vacuum. No fraction helps at equal margin

| vs | internal fraction | margin | buckling SF | yield SF | crush (Pa) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P2-E | 0 | 0 | 0.234 | 0.389 | 47730 |
| P2-E | 0.10 | -0.111 | 0.260 | 0.432 | 42957 |
| P2-E | 0.25 | -0.333 | 0.312 | 0.518 | 35798 |
| P2-E | 0.50 | -1.000 | 0.468 | 0.777 | 23865 |
| P2-A | 0.50 | -1.000 | 0.462 | 0.869 | 23865 |

Net load and gross lift both multiply by (1-f): load because only the pressure difference crushes, lift because the internal gas at the same temperature weighs f of the displaced air. SF at fixed structure rises as 1/(1-f), exactly the factor by which the floatable mass falls. Rescale the structure to restore the margin and the SF gain cancels to the last digit. No internal fraction helps SF at equal margin. This lever does nothing.

## Lever 4, cluster. The same lever as radius, with a systems penalty

| vs | n | each R (m) | margin | buckling SF | yield SF | crush (Pa) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P2-E | 1 | 10 | 0 | 0.234 | 0.389 | 47730 |
| P2-E | 4 | 6.30 | -0.637 | 0.589 | 0.617 | 47730 |

| P2-E | 27 | 3.33 | -2.491 | 2.105 | 1.166 | 47730 |
| P2-A | 27 | 3.33 | -2.491 | 2.077 | 1.304 | 47730 |

Total lift is held equal. Each small sphere buckles as 1/r^2, so the cluster gains n^(2/3) in buckling and n^(1/3) in yield, and pays n^(1/3) in face and core area, plus 100 kg systems per sphere under the mass rules. At n = 27 both SFs pass 1, the only points in this card that do, on a structure three and a half times the lift. It does not float.

## The best lever

Shrinking the sphere, directly or by clustering, is the strongest lever in the card: buckling SF x9 at n = 27, x4 at 5 m. Its size is real geometry, not allowance spread. It is also unaffordable: every point that gains SF loses float margin, and no lever in this card reaches SF 1 while floating. Geometry moves the gap; it does not close it. The material gap table, MAINLINE-008, remains the lever that must. Neither MAINLINE-007 nor 008 has filed, so lever 1 is not re-run on their rows. Open issues are in NFI.json.
