## The published record

No opened source is a wrinkling test of thin SR-200 beryllium on CR III 5056 3/8-1.0 honeycomb with the processed bond.

NASA CR-1457 (1969) is a public design handbook, not a new coupon series. For honeycomb cores its equation 2.2-8 is σ_wr = 0.82 sqrt(E_c E_f t_f / t_c) / (1 + 0.64 K_δ), with K_δ = δ E_c / (t_c F_c). F_c is the lower of flatwise core compression, flatwise core tension, and the core-to-facing bond. Perfect faces, K_δ = 0, give Q = 0.82, about 5 percent under the Bartelds and Mayers 0.86. When initial waviness is unknown, equation 2.2-14 uses Q = 0.33 as a lower bound. The handbook calls that a knockdown of approximately 0.4, and it says the equation is a best-available guide, not a substitute for a test of the selected sandwich. Figure 2.2-5, which justifies 0.33, plots Reference 2-7 and Reference 2-10. Elastic η = 1. This coefficient is admissible with a named limit: it is the 1969 unknown-waviness floor for older honeycomb coupons, not a measurement of this face, this core, or this bond. It authorizes 0.33. It does not authorize any higher installed value.

FPL Report 1810-A (Norris, Boller, and Voss, June 1953) is Reference 2-7. Faces are tempered spring steel sheet, 0.01 inch, proportional limit above 200,000 psi, modulus near 30 million psi. Two resin-treated paper honeycomb cores. Core A cell size in the R direction is 0.24 inch, six thicknesses from 3/8 inch to 2-3/8 inch, five specimens each, coupons 4 inches by 2 inches, plaster ends. The thinner groups failed by shear instability; CR-1457 discarded those. The average facing stress they used for the remaining half-wave check is 43,900 psi, and the computed half-wave 0.249 inch matches the 0.24 inch cell. Core B cell size is 0.17 inch, measured on one specimen, seven thicknesses, three specimens at 3/8 inch, five at 1/2 and 5/8 inch, two at 7/8 inch, and six at each remaining thickness. The bond is unnamed; the report attributes scatter to the tensile strength of the bond. Core density was not taken from the prose. The reported E_R of 0.99 psi and E_T of 4.53 psi, read between machine heads, are not the flatwise modulus normal to the facings that equation 2.2-8 uses, so no classical Q was computed. Not admissible for this hull. The replacing test is the elastic coupon on the actual sandwich.

FPL Report 2171 (Jenkinson and Kuenzi, December 1959) is Reference 2-10, aluminum and stainless-steel faces. CR-1457 says those points were kept only where the plasticity factor E_t/E_f was at least 0.85 and flatwise tension was at least flatwise compression. This seat did not open the report. Guessed Forest Service paths did not return it, and no other copy was found. Gauge, core, density, cell, bond, stress, and specimen count are not stated here. Not admissible. Opening the report would still leave a transfer limit unless the coupons are this beryllium sandwich.

Vogan and Grant, NTRS 19730016169, developed beryllium honeycomb from 1 July 1967 to 27 February 1972. The edgewise coupons use two 0.020 inch cross-rolled beryllium faces cut from 0.020 inch by 36 inch by 96 inch sheet, proportional limit about 35 ksi at room temperature. The core is beryllium ingot foil, not 5056 aluminum. A radiograph caption says 0.25 inch cell and a panel caption says 0.5 inch height. An earlier screen core, 1/4 inch cell, 0.005 inch foil, about 5.3 lb/ft³, is not assigned to these coupons, because the edgewise prose does not restate cell, density, or thickness. The bond is an Ag-27Cu-10Sn braze, alloy code 7, not the programme film. Specimens are about 3 inches by 4 inches, MIL-STD-401A short column, ends filled with epoxy or Glasrock. Average facing stress is 79.8 to 91.2 ksi at room temperature, 77.0 to 80.8 ksi at 300°F, and 60.2 to 72.0 ksi at 600°F. Every failure is after plastic deformation. ## What P2 can carry

The supported Q today is 0.33, one design value, admissible only with the limit above. The range is not a band from 0.33 to 0.82. The classical cap 0.82 remains the comparison anchor inside 063's model. It is not an installed test value.

What drives 0.33 rather than 0.82 is unknown initial face waviness. The ratio 0.33/0.82 is about 0.40, the handbook's knockdown. Core density sits inside both the stress and K_δ through E_c, and through F_c when the core sets the flatwise strength. The bond sets F_c only when it is weaker than the core. Neither density nor the bond is what the handbook changed when it wrote 0.33. The plot also dropped poor bonds and strongly inelastic failures before that floor was drawn, so 0.33 is not a poor-bond penalty.

At the supported Q, face wrinkling binds in every case below. The model's wrinkling line is BETA times Q times the square root, with BETA = 0.682327803828082, the root of b³ + b = 1. That extra factor is left as found. It is named in NFI.json. It was not edited.
