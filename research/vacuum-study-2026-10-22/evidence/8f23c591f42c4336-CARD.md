Unit `vacuum-mainline063-favorable-spread-1020`. QUESTIONABLE inputs. PROJECTION targets. No qualified hull safety factor.

The arithmetic is 061's `model61.py`, copied byte for byte, plus `model63.py`, which changes only the five selectors so one assumption can sit at the handbook value while the others stay at the favorable endpoint. Unreverted, it matches `model61.py`. Both bases reproduce 061's concept-closure face-density factors for R 20 and R 30, ISA and optimistic, to the digits in 061's RESULTS, and the favorable-endpoint payloads to 0.1 kg. The run is simjob scope `simjob-20261020t141536-2e6f2680cba4`, one core, 222 s. No finite-element run.

A face-density factor is the smallest divisor of 1850 kg/m³ that brings the reoptimized concept payload to at least zero. Share is `ln(F_swap / F_endpoint) / ln(F_handbook / F_endpoint)`. Payload is lift minus the resized bill minus 061's growth. Concept growth is 15% structure, 30% hardware, 20% thermal. TRL 7 is 5% hardware and 6% battery.

## Each input

**Gamma.** Favorable law: `0.2 + 0.4 min(Vc, 2)`, capped at 1. Handbook law, from 017b: CR-1457, 0.20 below Vc 2 and 1 at or above, the 1969 sandwich-hemisphere design value from two 1964 tests. Product form is an analysis knockdown, not a measured Be/CR-III sphere. The favorable ramp is not admissible. It is the surrogate 061 already named, not a test. The handbook 0.20 is admissible with a named limit: a hemisphere design value, not a 20–30 m shell of this sandwich. At the favorable point the ramp is about three times the handbook step.

**Q.** Favorable 0.82 is the classical perfect-face cap. Handbook 0.33 is the design coefficient 017b cites, unchanged since 1969, and 017b's reading of CR-1457 equation 2.2-12/13 at K_delta 2.32. Measured coefficients in that note run 0.125 to 0.82. Neither number is a Be face on CR-III 5056 3/8-1.0. The design 0.33 is admissible with that limit. The cap 0.82 is not admissible as an installed value.

**Bond film.** Favorable 0.07324 kg/m² per interface is 0.015 lb/ft². Baseline 0.14647 kg/m² is 0.03 lb/ft². 017b calls AF 163-2U at 0.015 lb/ft² today's film in the 028 joining endpoint, and its history lists about 73 g/m² for 2006–2009 and about 147 g/m² for 1975. Product form is an areal film weight, not a weighed curved Be scarf. 040 assigns no installed strength from film weight. Both figures are admissible with that limit. They are historical areal weights, not this joint.

**Modulus.** Favorable 293.6 GPa is 040's `E_full_Pa` on the SR-200 rolled-sheet row. 017b's history lists 293 GPa in 1971 and 290 GPa in 2024, and it states today's Materion SR-200 sheet modulus as 290 GPa. The 0.6 GPa between 293.6 and 293 is not tied to a page in the files this card read. Handbook 290 GPa is admissible with a named limit: a sheet scalar, not the orthotropic formed face at the gauges and temperatures FE-NEXT requires. The favorable 293.6 GPa is not admissible as a distinct better allowable until that 0.6 GPa has a source. It carries 0.3% of the spread either way.

**Strength.** Favorable 379.2 MPa is 040's `strength_full_Pa`. 017b says one 1967 lot reached 379 MPa in compression, and that SR-200's 345 MPa minimum tensile yield has stood since 1966 and is taken as compressive. Product form is rolled sheet, tensile minimum or one lot, transferred to compression. It is not biaxial, not formed, and not the thin gauge of a resized face. The 345 MPa figure is admissible with that transfer limit. The 379.2 MPa figure is not admissible as a design allowable. It also carries none of this gap.

## What would settle the carriers

Gamma and Q carry the spread. Film is a small third. Modulus and strength are not the direction.

The cheapest named check on Q is 030e's ground-first item 5: a honeycomb wrinkling coupon, beryllium on CR-III 5056 3/8-1.0. The same register prices it as T3, an initial 6 to 18 kUSD, with standards C364, C273 and C365, and a biaxial follow-on of 18 to 45 kUSD. That coupon would decide whether 0.33 or something closer to 0.82 is even plausible. It would not by itself make Q admissible for the sphere.

Gamma's named calculation is ground-first item 1: an imperfect, geometrically nonlinear sandwich-sphere finite-element series at Vc 1.06 and 0.89. This card's favorable geometries sit at Vc 0.99 to 1.07, so that is the right neighborhood. 030e's register, at its 2026-10-20T02:27 cutoff, did not take a MAINLINE-029 result as filed, and this card assumes none. The physical article in that same list is T7, a closed sandwich surrogate shell at 30 to 90 kUSD. FE-NEXT says the first admissible finite-element case is one measured curved article, with a released orthotropic sheet law and a measured core through crushing and mixed load, predicted without tuning to the collapse load. A coefficient swap and an unmeasured shell do not meet that bar. 062 ran none of it.

Film's weighed curved sealed panel is ground-first item 3 and T2, 12 to 35 kUSD. It is the right test for the 0.015 versus 0.03 lb/ft² choice, and it is not what the float rests on. T1, finished SR-200 compression at 5 to 15 kUSD, would settle the 345 versus 379 MPa question. That question does not move R 30.

## The answer

P2's apparent float rests on using the gamma ramp and Q=0.82 at the same time. Take either one back to the handbook and the R 30 TRL 7 surplus is gone. Take both back and 95.7% of the density-factor gap between the favorable endpoint and the handbook returns. The 379.2 MPa lot can be deleted from the optimism account. The 293.6 GPa modulus is a 0.3% item, and its source is not cleanly the 1971 figure of 293 GPa.

From the handbook value, neither carrier closes R 30 on its own. Gamma does not close at a multiplier of 5, which is the cap that lifts the 0.20 step to 1. Q does not close at 0.82. Film does not close when its mass is driven down. Strength does not close. Modulus closes only in ISA, at a factor 2.157, which is 626 GPa, past the 306.9 GPa polycrystal limit 017b cites. Optimistic weather does not close on modulus at all.

So the improvement that actually closes the handbook hull in this model is the one 061 already assigned: face density divided by 2.1435 in ISA, to 863 kg/m³, or by 2.2480 in optimistic weather, to 823 kg/m³. That is a different face, not a grounding of gamma or Q. If gamma and Q stay at the favorable laws, the concept hull at R 30 still needs density divided by 1.0593 or 1.1001, to 1746 or 1682 kg/m³. Those smaller cuts are available only while the two coefficients stay optimistic.

The materials direction is to measure Q on this sandwich and to run the named gamma shell, expecting those measurements to remove the float, and to treat a face near 860 kg/m³ as the target if the coefficients come back at the handbook. None of these figures is a certification.
