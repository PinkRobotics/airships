# MAINLINE-075 — the equibiaxial rule on this sphere, and the thermal interface

Unit vacuum-mainline075-wrinkle-exemption-1022. QUESTIONABLE / PROJECTION. No qualified hull safety factor. No finite-element run and no simjob. The only evaluation is the model expression at the version-10 design point.

Design point, from the version-9 closure carried unchanged in version 10: R 30 m, optimistic concept, face 3.6681482955475604 mm at 914.2756147739346 kg/m3, core 167.03346903487332 mm. Modulus 293.6 GPa. Q 0.33. γ 0.20. Direct evaluation at those gauges gives wrinkling 1.0000000003868577 and global buckling 1.000000008796374.

## Admissibility, stated first

Equation (2.2-15) is admissible with a named limit. The thermal interface formula is not admissible as a sourced law. It is a model estimate.

## The wrinkle beside the sphere

CR-1457 does not print a face-wrinkling half-wavelength for honeycomb. The honeycomb design equations are stresses. Equation (2.2-14), unknown waviness and elastic η = 1, is the uniaxial lower bound. Equation (2.2-15) is Rx³ + Ry³ = 1 when both facings are in compression, and (2.2-16) defines each R as the applied compression divided by the critical value acting alone. Neither equation contains a length. Figure 2.2-6 is the initial-waviness ratio δ/tc, not the buckle length.

The solid-or-foam section does define a dimensionless ζ as the parameter that involves the core moduli, the core thickness and the buckle wavelength, and equation (2.2-2) is minimized with respect to ζ. Section 2.2.2 says that development applies to solid or foam cores. It does not print the map from ζ to a length, and the honeycomb equations that this hull uses do not use ζ.

What the handbook does say, section 1.2: face wrinkling is a localized mode, short wavelengths in the facings, not confined to individual cells, and it strains the core through its thickness. Except for shear crimping, general-instability wavelengths are considerably larger than face-wrinkling wavelengths.

At this design point the lengths that are known are:

| Length | Metres | Beside R = 30 m |
|---|---:|---:|
| Face thickness | 0.0036681482955475604 | R/tf = 8178.513 |
| Core cell, 3/8 in | 0.009525 | R/cell = 3149.606 |
| Core thickness | 0.16703346903487332 | R/tc = 179.604723 |

The cell is 2.596 face thicknesses. A wrinkle on the scale of one cell is short beside R. That comparison uses the cell, which the handbook distinguishes from the wrinkle. It is not a calculated half-wavelength. DOI 10.2514/3.5568 is axial buckling of imperfect monocoque cylinders and does not give this wavelength. DOI 10.2514/3.1932 is a general shell solver and does not give it either.

Curvature does not split the two membrane principals. On this sphere both are compressive and equal: the model stress pR/(4 tf) is the same in both directions, because the two faces share pR/(2t). Equation (2.2-15) then returns (1/2) to the power 1/3, which is 0.7937005259840998. The exemption in which one principal membrane stress is not compressive does not apply. The thermal bending term is a gradient through the face thickness. It raises the demand. It does not change the sign of one membrane principal.

## Verdict on (2.2-15)

Admissible with a named limit. The limit is that the handbook's own half-wavelength is not printed, so the separate question, whether the wrinkle is long beside R, is unchecked on paper. The flat-panel interaction is the sentence the handbook gives for biaxial compression, and this membrane is equibiaxial.

The rule is not shown to fail to transfer. The standing targets therefore stay the equibiaxial pair: 896.8181102430983 kg/m3 at concept and 1030.8460016942847 kg/m3 at TRL 7, both set by R 20 optimistic. The pre-2.2-15 targets, 993 and 1146 kg/m3, are what stand if that named limit is later closed against the rule. Both pairs are QUESTIONABLE.

T3 has to reproduce the curvature, so a measured wrinkle length can be set beside R 20 m and R 30 m, the production cell of 3/8 in, and the design gauges, faces 3.668148 mm and core 167.033 mm on the R 30 optimistic concept article. A flat coupon does not close this limit.

## The thermal interface

model61.py and model63.py compute the same interface. In the copies used for 073 the lines match.

kb = 0.5 × 67×10⁹ × ρ_core / 2700 = 198747601.12135726 Pa.

ρ_core is 16.018463373960138 kg/m3. The 67 GPa and 2700 kg/m3 are an aluminum modulus and density. The one-half factor is in the code and is not derived on the page.

The reference temperature is 366.4833333333333 K, which is 200 °F. The service swing and the cure increment are

ΔT = 1.002 × max(|366.4833333333333 − Tmax|, |366.4833333333333 − Tmin|)

ΔTc = 1.002 × max(366.4833333333333 − Tmin, 0)

Tmax and Tmin are log-interpolated in core thickness from the R 30 table. At this core they are 302.244612719857 K and 270.8136848442734 K. The core is inside the table, 0.003 m to 4.5 m, so the interpolation is not clamped. ΔT = ΔTc = 95.86098778603802 K. The same call in model61 and model63 returns the same four temperatures.

The interface stress is

τ = 12.3×10⁻⁶ × ΔT × √(2 kb G)

with G the core shear modulus 46884349.59354485 Pa. The 12.3×10⁻⁶ is an aluminum expansion, not a measured bond coefficient. The allowable in the margin is 25 psi, written as 25 × 6894.757293168361 Pa.

At the design point, τ = 160963.31386619766 Pa and the margin is 1.0708584968155688. The separate cure stress, kb × 12.3×10⁻⁶ × ΔTc × tc / (2 tf), is 5335504.946858327 Pa. It enters the wrinkling demand. It is not τ.

None of kb, the 200 °F reference, the 1.002 factor, the 25 psi allowable, or this τ is in CR-1457, DOI 10.2514/3.5568, or DOI 10.2514/3.1932. The temperatures are a transferred table. The stress is a model estimate.

Face density does not enter τ, ΔT, or the wrinkling allowable. The same gauges at 1850 kg/m3 give the same thermal margin, 1.0708584968155688, and the same wrinkling margin, 1.0000000003868577. Wrinkling binds first at every face density on this article. There is no density at which the thermal interface binds before wrinkling, and no re-optimization is required to see that. The thermal law does not move the standing target.
