For the projected orthotropic calculation, [Nemeth and Mikulas, NASA/TP-2009-215778](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20090029956) provides the shell formulation. It does not validate applying the old isotropic knockdown to an arbitrary laminate. Steeves' [Caltech thesis, Table 6.3](https://thesis.library.caltech.edu/8902/1/JohnSteeves_Thesis_2015_Final.pdf) models an M55J/epoxy lamina with E1=340, E2=6, G12=4.2 GPa and nu12=.35 at a stated approximate 50% Vf. Transferring its transverse constants to Toray's different 60% Vf composite is an explicit unvalidated constitutive assumption.

CFRP is not generally ductile or immune to brittle impact damage. Delamination and subsequent compression failure must be assessed separately; a monolithic ceramic Weibull fit cannot supply their probability. [NASA/TP-2016-219211](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20160010546/downloads/20160010546.pdf) studies this problem. A fibre-reinforced tube may tolerate local damage better, but the present evidence does not quantify that advantage for P3.

The prior 043 source audit and 045 tube-test records are retained in `intake/043/SOURCES.md` and `intake/045/p3damage.json`. The latter distinguishes C-ring/sectored tensile flexure from axial compression. Its 299 MPa/m4.6 and 192 MPa/m5.5 scales are geometry/population-specific; their effective stressed area is not supplied for an ice-contact transfer.
