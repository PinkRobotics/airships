## 1. The CR-1457 rule, and what it rests on

NASA CR-1457 (Sullins, Smith and Spier, 1969), https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19700004831. Local extract `unpublished-local-ref:ee2839a020854191 (OCR). Section 6.2.

The theory is Yao, Journal of the Aerospace Sciences, March 1962, ref 6-1, reformulated by Plantema, Sandwich Construction, Wiley, 1966, ref 6-2. Assumptions stated there: isotropic equal faces, face bending about their own middle surfaces neglected, core through-thickness stiffness infinite, core in-plane stiffness negligible, isotropic transverse shear, and R/tc much greater than 1. With isotropic core shear, Yao's sphere result is the axial sandwich-cylinder equations with the knockdown included. sigma_cr = Yd * Kc * sigma_0. Kc = 1 - Vc/4 when Vc <= 2, and Kc = 1/Vc when Vc > 2. sigma_crimp = h^2 * Gc / (2 * tf * tc).

Page 6-7: Vc = 2 divides stiff and moderately stiff cores from the weak-core region. Sensitivity to initial imperfections decreases as Vc goes from 0 to 2. Weak-core constructions are said not to be influenced by initial imperfections, and a knockdown of unity can be applied. The handbook says a continuous transitional knockdown that follows core rigidity is beyond its scope.

Section 6.2.2, page 6-12: hemispheres use Yd = 0.20 when Vc < 2 and Yd = 1 when Vc >= 2. Ellipsoids and torispheres use 0.35 below 2 and 1 at and above 2.

Kc is continuous at Vc = 2, because both expressions equal 1/2. Yd is not. MAINLINE-014 places the gamma step on Vc > 2, with the Kc inequality. Page 6-12 places Yd = 1 at Vc >= 2. No row scored here is exactly Vc = 2. The step is not smoothed.

Dome tests, section 6.2.1.2: the handbook calls them very scarce and the method best-available, not very reliable, a rough guideline. Final designs must be substantiated by test. The useful set is Gonzalez and Patton, North American Rockwell report SDL 468, May 1964, ref 6-4, Saturn S-II 55-inch bulkheads. The hemisphere factor 0.20 is explicitly except where shear crimping occurs.

The cylinder evidence cited for unity is axial compression, section 4.2, Figs 4.2-4 and 4.2-5. References: March and Kuenzi, FPL Report 1830, revised December 1957 (4-2), and a Hexcel Products status summary, 25 July 1963 (4-11). All but one test exceed the gamma-1 prediction. The exception failed at 0.86 of predicted. Individual Vc values are not tabulated. The class is Vc >= 2. Section 2.3 says no knockdown is required because shear crimping is insensitive to imperfections, and that neglecting face bending is conservative and can be considerable. These are not externally pressurised spheres. The transfer is QUESTIONABLE.

## 3. Two continuous curves

Optimistic, PROJECTION: gamma(Vc) = 0.20 + 0.40 * min(Vc, 2). It is 0.20 at Vc = 0 and 1 at Vc >= 2. The endpoints are the handbook's words on page 6-7. The straight line is not in the handbook. It does not reduce the shear-crimp branch.

Conservative, QUESTIONABLE: gamma(Vc) = 0.20 at every Vc. This is equation 6.2-13 with the crimp exception withheld, because the sphere tests are scarce and unity was not read from a sphere series at Vc > 2.

Both replace kd. They are not stacked on 0.20, 0.90 or 0.1407. The design pressure is gamma(Vc) * Kc * (4 * tf / R) * sigma_0. On the crimp branch that equals gamma times the bare crimp pressure. Face yield, crush, wrinkling and the bare crimp formula are otherwise unchanged. Kc is the handbook branch function. It is not smoothed.

The single cylinder ratio 0.86 is not scored as a third curve.
