# MAINLINE-013 ultralight core properties, the gap below 8 kg/m3 — ESTIMATE

Unit vacuum-mainline013-ultralight-cores-1019. Order ord-admiral-rr2-mainline013-rotated-opus-1019. Status ESTIMATE. Every sourced value is QUESTIONABLE; scale-up claims and extrapolations are PROJECTION. Nothing here is a certification. Started 2026-10-19T23:20:07-07:00 (order read); the end time is in RESULTS.json. Those times are measurements, not limits.

## The answer

No measured core at or below 10 kg/m3 passes all three checks with 6061 faces on the P2 float ceiling. 76 cores were evaluated, 47 of them at or below 10 kg/m3, and none in that band passes.

The binding property is the wrinkling product Ec*Gc for continuum cores (foams, aerogels, lattices), and Ec alone for honeycomb. The best measured core at or below 10 kg/m3 is the hollow alumina octet nanolattice of Meza, Das and Greer (2014) at 5.5 kg/m3. On the least-factor split it needs 5.84e3 times its Ec*Gc, which is 18 times on wrinkling stress. On the buckling split it needs 1.7e4 times.

In 008b's gap band, 1 to 4 kg/m3, the best wrinkling is nanoporous graphene (Kashani 2019) at 3 kg/m3. It needs 7.6e4 times its product, 42 times on stress. The best crimp there needs 6.3 times its Gc, and the best structural crush needs 11.9 times its strength (Zheng 2016 hierarchical Ni-P at 1 kg/m3).

Crush is met at 5.3 kg/m3 by the hierarchical hollow alumina nanolattice of Meza et al. (2015), margin 1.34. That value is a figure read of about ±25%, and the density is inferred from relative density. It is the lightest sourced core with a structural strength above p/2. Crimp is met nowhere at or below 10 kg/m3 on the least-factor split; the nearest is 1.36 times short (Meza 2014, 5.5 kg/m3). On the buckling split crimp is met at 0.3, 5.3 and 5.5 kg/m3, but that split's core at 0.3 kg/m3 is 6 m thick.

The lightest core in this table that passes all three is a vitreous carbon octet micro-lattice at 190 kg/m3: crush 214, crimp 11.4, wrinkling 5.78 on the product. It comes second-hand, through Zhang et al. (2019) quoting Jacobsen et al. (2011); the primary paper was not opened. At 190 kg/m3 the float-ceiling core is 9.2 mm thick and the shell's buckling SF is 0.020 at kd 0.2, against 0.234 at 16 kg/m3, so passing the local checks there buys nothing globally. This card sourced no core between 53 and 190 kg/m3, so the true lightest passing core sits somewhere at or below 190 kg/m3 (NFI-013-MID-DENSITY).

PROJECTION. An ideal stretch-dominated lattice (Ashby 2006, eq 5.1: E = rho*S/3, with S = Es/rho_s of the strut) with Gc = Ec/2 passes wrinkling and crimp on the least-factor split from these densities:
- K13D2U-grade pitch-fibre struts: 1.34 kg/m3
- unidirectional CNT yarn: 2.46 kg/m3 (PROJECTION material)
- beryllium: 4.82 kg/m3
- ALD alumina: 25.3 kg/m3
- 6061: 55.9 kg/m3

The measured alumina nanolattices reach 2.8 to 4.2 percent of their own ideal modulus. At 5 kg/m3 a real lattice would need 38 percent of ideal with K13D2U-grade struts, 63 percent with CNT yarn and 98 percent with beryllium. So the gap below 8 kg/m3 is two gaps: a lattice-efficiency factor of about 9 to 23, and strut materials stiffer per kilogram than beryllium.

## The needs at each density

Case: 6061-T6 faces, P2 float ceiling, kd 0.2. The rows are 007's two splits, rebuilt at each core's own density. The B split maximises buckling. The Q split maximises the lesser of buckling and yield. The splits coincide above 9.599 kg/m3, and below it Q holds the equal-factor separation of 0.18907 m. Face stress is p*R/(4*tf). All three needs and the face stress are independent of kd.

Crush: core compressive strength 47730.4 Pa, which is p/2 (MAINLINE-005b's model).

Crimp: Gc >= p*R*tc/(2*h^2), the CR-1457 crimp pressure set to p. On the Q split this is 2.52 MPa at every density up to 9.6 kg/m3, then 2.63 MPa at 10, 4.20 MPa at 16 and 8.37 MPa at 32. On the B split below 9.6 kg/m3 it falls with density, to 26 kPa at 0.1 kg/m3, 263 kPa at 1 and 2.10 MPa at 8. Gc of 2.45 MPa (B) to 4.86 MPa (Q at 0.1) puts general instability back on its moderate branch (Vc = 2, 007b).

Wrinkling, continuum form (CR-1457 eq 2.2-5 with Q 0.50 and the equibiaxial factor 0.6823 from eq 2.2-6), Ec*Gc needed on the Q split:
- 0.1 kg/m3: 1.48e16 Pa^2, face 357 MPa, a geometric-mean modulus of 122 MPa
- 1 kg/m3: 1.71e16 (374 MPa, 131 MPa)
- 2 kg/m3: 2.03e16 (396 MPa, 142 MPa)
- 4 kg/m3: 2.93e16 (448 MPa, 171 MPa)
- 8 kg/m3: 7.31e16 (608 MPa, 270 MPa)
- 10 kg/m3 and above: 1.16e17 (709 MPa, 341 MPa)

On the B split it is 1.16e17 to 1.17e17 at every density, because the face stays near 710 MPa. 007's advisory uniaxial product, (2 sigma)^3/E, is 0.357 of these numbers.

Wrinkling, honeycomb form (eq 2.2-14, coefficient 0.33): Ec of 10.3 to 50.8 GPa on the Q split from 0.1 to 8 kg/m3, 77.4 GPa at 10, 48.1 GPa at 16 and 31.9 GPa at 24. No honeycomb in the table comes within two orders of magnitude of its own need; the best is 216 times short, at 25.6 kg/m3.

Second level, carried as a column only. The classical coefficients are 0.76 for the continuum form (NASA/CR-1999-208994, Table 3, theoretical upper bound) and 0.82 for honeycomb (CR-1457 eq 2.2-12, K_delta 0). They cut the product need to 0.285 of the design value and the honeycomb Ec need to 0.162. That moves the best gap at or below 10 kg/m3 from 5.84e3 to 1.66e3 on the product, and the best honeycomb gap from 216 to 35 on Ec. No verdict changes.

A fourth local mode, reported beside the three: intracell dimpling (CR-1457 eq 2.1-4, straight-line biaxial rule eq 2.1-6) allows cells up to 3.5 mm on the B split and above 9.6 kg/m3, rising to 9.8 mm on the Q split at 0.1 kg/m3. Every honeycomb cell in the table fails it except the 1/8-inch cell (1.11).

The 6061 face is past its 276 MPa QUESTIONABLE yield on every row (face stress 357 to 710 MPa at p; the yield SF is at most 0.777, 007's all-face limit). The wrinkling numbers are the elastic forms (eta 1) at that stress, so they flatter the faces where the face has already yielded.

## Method

Each property came from a page or PDF actually opened, with every DOI checked against Crossref. Four sourcing sub-workers of this seat gathered them, one per family: metal lattices, ceramic and carbon lattices, aerogels, and catalog honeycomb and foams. vet_sources.py re-checked all 21 DOIs independently: 21 match (source_check.json). The owner hand-checked the following against their sources:
- Kashani 2019 Table S1, and its sentence "the consistent elastic moduli of tension and compression for each sample" (Europe PMC full text). That sentence is the basis for using its tensile modulus as Ec.
- the Hexcel CR III 5052 and 5056 rows
- the CR-1457 equations, read from page images

build_cores.py copies the values and applies stated curation only. Shear: no source measured a shear modulus for any core below 10 kg/m3. Gc is therefore DERIVED as Ec/2, the largest an isotropic core with nu >= 0 can have, which is optimistic, in line with COMMON's optimistic estimates. At nu = 1/3 every product falls by 0.75; no verdict moves. Honeycomb uses the smaller of its L and W plate-shear moduli. Strength: a value taken at large strain, of undefined strain, by indentation or in tension can fail the crush check but cannot pass it. This covers Qiu's stress at 80 percent strain, Si's undefined "max stress", Xu's ultimate stress, Basotect at 40 percent, Zhu at 50 percent and Kashani's indentation and tension values. Excluded, with reasons, in RESULTS not_evaluated:
- an EPS sheet whose shear modulus exceeds its Young's modulus
- an abstract read through a summarising tool
- a deliberately crushed acoustic foam
- Wikipedia-only aerographite values
- Chen 2017's values, whose sample density is unsourced
- an unpaired Meza context point

compute.py rebuilds 007's rows and reproduces its sealed numbers before judging any core:

## Scaling exponents with density (sources in RESULTS scaling, all QUESTIONABLE)

Metallic micro-lattices: hollow Ni-P, E ~ rho^2 with E/Es about 3 (rho/rho_s)^2, and collapse strength ~ rho^2.06, bending-dominated (Maloney 2013, 1 to 55 kg/m3; Schaedler 2011 abstract). Hierarchical (fractal) Ni-P octet: strength ~ rho^1.3, stretch-dominated; first-order octet Ni-P below 0.2 percent: strength exponent over 2.5 (Zheng 2016).

Ceramic and carbon lattices: hollow alumina octet, E ~ rho^1.61 and strength ~ rho^1.76, matching neither ideal regime (Meza 2014, 6.3 to 258 kg/m3). Hierarchical hollow alumina: E = 0.015 Es rho_rel^1.04 and strength = 0.026 sigma_ys rho_rel^1.17, stretch-dominated (Meza 2015, 0.3 to 5.3 kg/m3). Glassy carbon tetrahedral: E ~ rho^1.67 and strength ~ rho^2.0 (Bauer 2016, quoted second-hand). Printed graphene micro-lattice: E ~ rho^2.5, bending-dominated (Zhu 2015, 16 to 123 kg/m3).

Aerogels: graphene elastomers E ~ rho^2 to 2.2 (Qiu 2012, 0.56 to 6.62 kg/m3). Spongy graphene E ~ rho^2.21 (Wu 2015). Nanofibre aerogels E ~ rho^2 (Si 2014). Silica E ~ rho^3.6 to 3.7 and strength ~ rho^2.6 (Woignier 1989; Burger 1997; Ma 2000 simulation). Nanoporous graphene in tension E ~ rho_rel^1.6, strength ~ rho_rel^1.5 below 20 kg/m3 (Kashani 2019). CNT aerogels E ~ rho^3.1 (2025 compilation). Nanocellulose E ~ rho^1.53 (9 to 115 kg/m3).

Foams: EPS sigma(10%) = 1.98 rho^1.34 kPa and Ei = 0.0773 rho^1.47 MPa, 10 to 35 kg/m3 (Missirlis fit). EPS shear G = 20.1 T + 508 kPa, with T the EPS type (Vejelis 2008, from EPS50).

Theory (Ashby 2006, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. A 364, 15): stretch-dominated E/Es ~ rho_rel/3 and plastic strength ~ rho_rel/3 (an upper bound); bending-dominated E/Es ~ rho_rel^2, plastic collapse 0.3 rho_rel^1.5, elastic buckling 0.05 rho_rel^2.

Reading: every measured family below 10 kg/m3 scales at exponent 1.5 to 3.7 except the hierarchical alumina (1.04) and the fractal Ni-P (strength 1.3). An exponent near 2 or above is why the measured cores at or below 3 kg/m3 sit 4.9 to 12.4 orders of magnitude below the wrinkling product need.

## What would close it

The property is Ec*Gc for a continuum core, or a stiffer or thicker face, because the need scales as sigma_face^3/E_face. On these rows that need is set by the 6061 face at the float ceiling. 008b's face materials change it: the CNT-yarn face needs 3.25e14 Pa^2 on 007's 1 kg/m3 row in the advisory form. The factor is 1.7e3 to 5.8e3 on the product for the best measured core at 5.5 kg/m3, depending on the wrinkling level, and 2.2e4 to 7.6e4 in the 1 to 4 kg/m3 band. On the ideal-lattice reading, a stretch-dominated lattice of K13D2U-grade fibre struts at 38 percent of ideal efficiency would close it at 5 kg/m3. The best measured lattice efficiency is 4.2 percent (alumina), so the lattice-efficiency factor is about 9 even with such struts. NFI.json carries the rows. A checker runs behind this card.
