# MAINLINE056 — conditional crushable-core benchmark

**QUESTIONABLE / PROJECTION throughout. No certification. No P2 gamma released.**

The constitutive extension reduces the inherited 13.9569 MPa elastic result toward the paper's pressure prediction. With reconstructed indentation, the midsurface-radius case gives **6.159021 MPa (+2.140%)** against 6.03 MPa. The four indentation/radius cases with at least 96 meridional elements and contact penalty at least 30000 MPa/mm span **6.1590–6.5023 MPa**, or **+2.14% to +7.83%**. The preliminary 48-element, softer-contact pilot is excluded from that range and retained in the full results. This is a conditional scenario range, not a confidence interval or a calibrated error bound.

**The 6.03 MPa target is numerical, not measured.** EUCASS2023-367 predicts pressure collapse and validates separately against axial-compression tests. The source does not establish an experimentally validated pressure-shell capacity here. The answer to “does it reproduce the measured shell?” is therefore **not established**. The simulation comparison is much closer, but unresolved material and geometric inputs prevent a unique calibration. [Primary paper](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2023-367.pdf).

**Linear gap.** At 384 meridional elements, eight core layers and two layers per face, the dead-pressure eigenvalues are 30.218153614 MPa for outer radius 42.8 mm (+9.76445%) and 28.074312432 MPa for midsurface radius 42.8 mm (+1.97716%) against the paper's 27.53 MPa. Including follower-load stiffness gives 30.732852938 and 28.535199449 MPa respectively. `LINEAR.json` retains all three meshes and four eigenvalues per case. The radius reading explains much of the linear gap.

**Benchmark normalization.** The paper's pressure ratio is 6.03/27.53 = 0.219033781. The refined midsurface indentation pressure divided by the converged same-radius dead-pressure eigenvalue gives 0.219382777 (+0.159% relative to that ratio); using the follower-load eigenvalue gives 0.215839410. `RESULTS.json` keeps the denominator definition and first-turn ratio for every case. This numerical normalization is not a P2 lead gamma or a material validation.

Foam plastic yielding occurs first. For these two guarded reference cases, **face yield precedes the implied initial shear-strength crossing and the pressure turning point**. Plastic yielding is permitted by this constitutive model; it is not itself rupture. The foam shear screen is the assumed yield ellipse's virgin pure-shear strength, 0.989949 MPa, not a recovered PVC80 fracture allowable. Consequently, a physical shear-failure-versus-instability governing capacity cannot be assigned from these sources. At the refined indentation peak, maximum foam equivalent plastic strain is 0.664107, 1543 core integration points use extrapolation, resolved core shear is 1.426967 MPa, and minimum J is 0.398053.

**Material uncertainty.** The paper omits the foam hydrostatic strength and flow calibration. The declared zero-plastic-Poisson-ratio assumption and two alternative ellipses are separate scenarios. The printed foam densification slope exceeds its stated E=40 MPa; the constant-E monotone hardening fit cannot represent that entire curve. Later strains explicitly extrapolate the last retained slope. The steel plot's apparent factor-of-ten axis problem is unresolved: 750 MPa initial yield is a declared interpretation, not an established erratum.

**P2 disposition.** No trusted P2 gamma follows. It still needs identified directional honeycomb curves and mixed-mode failure, thin beryllium compression/fracture and bonding data, admissible finite-strain in-plane behavior, a realizable ribbon layout, imperfections/joints, and three-dimensional stability, followed by relevant experimental validation. The CHECK029 lead strength violations remain unresolved. A steel/PVC hemisphere cannot establish a honeycomb/beryllium knockdown by transfer alone.
