**Probability and reference size**

For a calibrated two-parameter scalar Weibull population:

```text
P_member = 1 − exp(−H_member)
H_member = (Qeffective/Qreference)(stress_max/scale_reference)^m
H_frame = sum(H_member_i)                  [independent series model]
P_frame = 1 − exp(−H_frame)
scale_member = scale_reference (Qreference/Qeffective)^(1/m)
```

Q is either volume or surface area; use consistent dimensions. A scale is the 63.2-percentile stress for its specified reference test/field. A mean implies `scale=mean/Gamma(1+1/m)` only under the assumed distribution. The conversion of a sample mean and fitted m is not a reconstruction of the original maximum-likelihood fit.

Compression is a **hypothetical scalar failure law** here. No candidate has a matched calibrated compression m/scale/reference size. Flexure m is never declared a compression measurement. The 3.9 GPa scenarios separately assume it is (a) the scale or (b) a mean, at complete-member size, 1 cm³ or 100 cm³ coupon size. Those reference volumes are deliberately assigned sensitivities, not manufacturer test dimensions. Uniform peak stress throughout each member gives an upper hazard within this monotone spatial-power model. Unequal 026b member peaks are summed, not replaced with an average force.

For exact-annulus stress integration the assumed longitudinal field is

```text
sigma(x,r,theta) = −c + [b_bow sin(pi x/L)+b_joint] (r/ro) cos(theta)
Veff = integral (max(±sigma,0)/peak)^m dV
Aeff = integral over inner+outer cylinder surfaces of the same normalized power
```

The sign selects compression or tension. End annuli, end contacts and circumferential stresses are omitted. The constant joint term is an envelope assumption. Volume quadrature uses 128 Gauss points along the member, 8 through the annulus, and 512 periodic angular midpoints. The localized 50 mm bow example is checked against 256×16×1,024 integration. All-compressive bow-only cases are also checked against an independent finite binomial expansion with analytic sine/cosine and radial moments.

For a rectangular four-point tensile bend reference:

```text
Leff = inner_span + (outer_span−inner_span)/(m+1)
Vref = b h Leff/[2(m+1)]
Aref = [b+h/(m+1)] Leff
```

SA uses documented 4×3 mm section but **assumed** 40/20 mm spans. The RBSC test has documented 30 mm three-point span; set inner_span=0. The surface expression includes the bottom and tensile side surfaces, assumed to share one population. It is not their full geometrical area. The alternative volume/surface calibrations from a single bend dataset must not be added or selectively chosen as if independently measured. Separately calibrated competing populations would have additive hazards.

Uniform positive-tension scenarios use fractions 1, 0.01 and 0.0001 of the full member's volume or both cylindrical surfaces. These are assumed effective stressed fractions, not guessed contact-patch dimensions. Hong's CVD coating scenarios use only the reported inner-surface calibration and preserve the separate material populations. The commercial CVD sheet and composite tensile fits lack the reference information needed for a justified full-size probability; they remain unscaled.

**Required strength, scatter and confidence**

For n identical series members and target P:

```text
h_target = −ln(1−P)
required_scale_member = stress [n/h_target]^(1/m)
required_scale_reference = stress [n Qeff/(h_target Qref)]^(1/m)
required_m = ln[n Qeff/(h_target Qref)] / ln(scale_reference/stress)
```

The last formula requires scale_reference>stress and fixed reference ratio. There is a tradeoff curve, not a unique required pair of scale and m. At member scale set Qeff/Qref=1; do not apply size reduction a second time. A tensile example uses the same algebra with positive tensile stress, not the compressive demand.

The allocated marginal probability is `1−(1−P)^(1/n)`. The union-bound allocation P/n avoids an independence assumption but still needs valid marginal probabilities. All headline targets allocate the whole risk budget to tube fracture, leaving no allowance for joints, instability, membrane failure or common-cause events. They are therefore not whole-vehicle reliability claims.

Zero failures in k independent representative tests gives one-sided 95% upper marginal bound `1−0.05^(1/k)`. Inverting it for the allocated member targets produces the enormous sample counts reported. This is an illustration of tail-validation difficulty, not a proposed billion-test campaign or a fitted confidence bound.

**Proof testing**

For an unchanged scalar strength S and perfect proof at stress sp, service at s≤sp has zero immediate conditional failure in the ideal truncation model. For s>sp, conditional failure is `1−exp(−[(s/scale)^m−(sp/scale)^m])`. Proof rejects have probability `1−exp(−(sp/scale)^m)`. A real proof must cover failure-relevant locations/directions and account for growth or damage during proof, later handling, environment and cycling. See PROOF.md; its costing is a class estimate, not a quote.
