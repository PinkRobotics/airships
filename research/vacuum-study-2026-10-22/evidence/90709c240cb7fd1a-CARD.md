## The answer

**037's screen says the hot cure breaks the bond.** AF 163-2U's datasheet cure is 250 F (121 C), 123 K above the cold night. In 037's screen that leaves 1063 kPa of face-core shear: factor 0.227 on the core's 241.3 kPa W strength. The brief's rough figure (about 900 kPa, 0.27) assumed about 100 K.

**That screen cannot be in equilibrium.** It holds each face at the core's own expansion. That needs a face force of 351 kN/m, which the core must carry back in its own plane: 703 kN/m for the two faces. The core's walls yield at 14.9 kN/m, 47 times less. Even 037's own 30.5 K swing asks 12 times. Three sources treat the core's in-plane stiffness as negligible:
- **CR-1457:** the facings "provide practically all of the over-all bending and in-plane extensional rigidity".
- **Hexcel AGU 075b:** "the in-plane stiffness of the honeycomb is negligible compared to that of the facing skins".
- **NASA TM-4585:** neglects the core's thermal force and moment.

**In the field the bond carries no mismatch shear.** In a continuous core the faces set the strain, and the cell walls take the mismatch by stretching. After the 250 F cure they carry:
- 102 MPa on a uniform cell, factor 2.24;
- 122 MPa in the single walls of CR-III's doubled-ribbon cell, factor 1.87.

Both factors are on the 227.5 MPa yield measured for 5052-H39 foil. The NASA co-cured-panel studies report this mismatch as cell-wall distortion and face waviness, not as debonds.

**At a free core edge the shear is real.** An unfilled splice, a closeout or a cut-out lets the core shed its force into the faces over about 16 mm. After the 250 F cure that is 207 kPa:
- factor 1.17 on the typical W strength;
- factor 0.83 on the 25 psi minimum average.

The W direction governs. An edge across the ribbon reads 1.23 / 1.01 on the L strength. The doubled-ribbon cell is softer and reads 1.23 / 0.88. The card keeps the uniform cell's lower figures.

**The cure's larger cost is in the faces, not the bond.** The core's pull leaves each face 4.4 MPa more compressed at 028b's lead, 1.5 percent of its working stress. That compression is balanced inside the wall, so it does not drive global buckling or crimping (this seat's argument). But it does pass through the face scarf.

028b fixes that scarf at 30:1, and its 10 MPa screen caps the face at 300.33 MPa. So the faces must thicken and the core shrink, from 23.2 to 20.9 mm:
- usage 0.972 to 1.052, beyond the named stretch;
- gamma needed 0.564 to 0.618;
- wrinkle needed 0.435 to 0.409.

At 037's floated point the scarf factor falls from 1.0064 to 0.9925. Any bond stress-free above 54 C does this.

**Verdict.** As designed, the bond survives in the field. It is marginal at free core edges on typical strength and fails there on minimum strength. The cure's face compression takes 028b's lead past its named stretch.

**The lightest sourced change adds 0.12 kg.** Two changes:
- Cure AF 163-2U at the 200 F (93 C) cycle its own datasheet gives (2-6 h). A free edge then reads 1.51 typical and 1.08 minimum.
- Lengthen the face scarf to 30.34:1, by 028's own sizing rule. That adds 0.12 kg of joint and restores usage 0.972 (gamma 0.564, wrinkle 0.440).

If filled splices are shown to carry the core's force, the 250 F cure needs only the scarf change: 30.44:1, 0.16 kg, usage 0.972.

A room-temperature or mid-swing cure needs no scarf change (0.990, 0.982) and adds nothing on paper. But it is priced at AF's areal weight (PROJECTION), and no source gives its honeycomb bond data.

**If 037's rigid screen is kept instead,** only a bond stress-free between 0.4 and 25.9 C passes on typical strength. On the minimum the window is 8.4-17.9 C.
- A room-temperature paste cured at 25 C reads 1.03 / 0.74.
- A cure at mid-swing (13 C) reads 1.83 / 1.31. Its small face compression leaves 028b at 0.982 on the 30:1 scarf.

No source gives honeycomb bond data for those pastes.

**The fixes the brief names fail on mass.** Every expansion-matched core with sourced properties is heavier than 16 kg/m3:

## 1. The residual state

Service is 037's coated band, 271.08 K (cold night) to 301.57 K (hot day), a 30.493 K swing. Each case takes its worst end. Aluminium is 23.6e-6 per K, beryllium 11.3e-6.

The two shear readings are both this seat's arithmetic:
- **037's screen**, reproduced exactly: lambda 3.0241 /m, 143.8 kPa for the day rise and 263.0 kPa for the swing, factor 0.9174. In it, tau = d_eps E_f t_f lambda.
- **The free-edge reading.** Honeycomb resists an equal biaxial strain only by stretching its walls, so K_biax = E_foil (rho*/rho_s)/2 on a uniform cell. That is 198.7 MPa for 5056 at 67 GPa and 2700 kg/m3. CR-III's doubled ribbon walls give 0.90 of that. Its bending modes (Gibson: E* about 1.5 Es (rho*/rho_s)^3, about 0.02 MPa) give way. At a free core edge the core keeps the faces' strain along the edge and contracts across it. The shear lag gives tau = 2 K2 d_eps sqrt(Gc/K2), with K2 = K_biax/2. This is a homogenised estimate (QUESTIONABLE).

The face compression is K_biax d_eps t_c / (2 t_f) on each face.

Factors are on the typical / minimum-average W strength. The last column is 037's floated point with the compression added.

## 3. The verdict

The lead's bond, AF 163-2U at 250 F on CR-III 5056, survives as designed wherever the core is continuous. CR-1457 itself gives the facings practically all of the in-plane rigidity, and 037's screen would need the core to carry 47 times its yield force.

Where the core has a free edge the bond is marginal: 1.17 on typical strength, 0.83 on minimum.

The cure's face compression costs 028b's lead more than the bond does. On 028b's fixed 30:1 scarf, usage goes from 0.972 to 1.052.

The lightest sourced change that restores SF 1 on this card's reading adds 0.12 kg:
- **AF 163-2U's own 200 F cure:** 1.08 at a free edge on minimum strength.
- **A 30.34:1 face scarf:** 028b back at 0.972.

A room-temperature or mid-swing cure adds nothing on paper, but it rests on projections.

If 037's rigid screen is kept instead, only a cold cure passes: a bond stress-free near 13 C (1.83 / 1.31). That is a process with no sourced honeycomb data.

Matched cores and soft interlayers do not work on this budget.

The open check is physical: a cured, cycled, curved Be/Al panel with a core splice and a face scarf, strain-gauged at both.
