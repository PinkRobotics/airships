**Curvature calculation — our inference from the supplied geometry.** Treating 2.04 m as centerline arc length, θ=L/R=0.204 rad=11.688°, chord=2R sin(θ/2)=2.036464 m, and sag relative to the chord is R[1−cos(θ/2)]=**51.9749 mm**. If 2.04 m instead means chord length, sag is 52.1560 mm. The difference does not change the conclusion. Sag is about 59 wall thicknesses; D/t≈281.04 and L/t≈2,317.39. It is intentional geometry, not an ordinary straightness tolerance.

Cold elastically bending a finished straight tube to this radius would nominally require outer-fiber strain rₒ/R=**1.237%**. The question’s QUESTIONABLE E=410 GPa monolithic screening value gives Eε≈**5.072 GPa**; the QUESTIONABLE CMC E=272 GPa surrogate gives ≈**3.365 GPa**. These linear-elastic extrapolations diagnose an untenable forming assumption; they are not predictions that a tube will elastically reach those stresses. The outer and inner fiber paths differ by Dθ≈50.47 mm. A manufacturable arc therefore needs shape formation before final ceramic densification, suitable deposition on a shaped removable form, or an explicitly redesigned segmented/jointed structure.

For an as-manufactured arc, metrology must bound deviations from the specified curved centerline, end tangent angle and position, local ovality, minimum wall, and end-face alignment. Straight catalogues do not provide those bounds. A second, accidental 1 mm eccentricity also adds a bending contribution N·0.001 m under an axial force N; the prescribed 52 mm sag must already be represented in the structural load path and cannot be silently erased.

**Transparent optimistic scalar for numerical screening.** Use QUESTIONABLE E=272 GPa, ρ=2,440 kg/m³, tensile PLS=128 MPa and tensile UTS=357 MPa from CVI (II). Add QUESTIONABLE compressive strength=**580 MPa only as a rounded, cross-material optimistic scenario**, derived from the separate Honeywell CVI value. It has no demonstrated joint distribution with those NASA properties. An optional 367 MPa compression sensitivity is also a cross-material scenario. If the solver requires a scalar Poisson ratio, ν=0.20 is **QUESTIONABLE, assumed, and unsourced**; do not imply an experimentally established isotropic CMC.

PLS is a **tensile** indicator of damage onset; it is not a tested compression limit, guarantee of hermeticity, or design allowable. UTS permits damaged, nonlinear response and cannot justify retaining the initial E all the way to failure. A proper tube model needs longitudinal, hoop, through-wall, and shear properties, bending stiffness, coupling, compression instability/delamination behavior, and the actual braid/layup. The 8-ply panel properties are not demonstrated in a 0.8803 mm wall. Using the scalar E in an isotropic local-buckling equation is a **PROJECTION**, not an established CMC shell stiffness.

In tension, matrix cracking and subsequent fiber bridging permit load beyond initial damage; in compression, fiber/tow instability, interface sliding and delamination can govern. The cited 2018 compression experiments observed quasistatic fiber debonding and bundle buckling. These different mechanisms preclude replacing a missing compression result with tensile PLS, tensile UTS, or a monolithic ceramic compression strength.

**Joining, contact, and quantity implications — engineering inferences.** At 1,046 target members the tube length totals approximately 2.134 km, with 2,092 tube ends before accounting for node sharing. None of the inspected sources establishes yield, inspected flaw distribution, proof-test survival, or dimensional interchangeability at this target quantity and geometry.

A bond or sleeve that extends a furnace tube does not automatically transfer the node forces, bending moments, or vacuum seal requirements of this structure. Joint eccentricity, overlap, adhesive/braze/shear transfer, thermal mismatch, and local wall reinforcement require explicit mass and stiffness accounting. Load introduced by a ring clamp, pin, flange, or tube end produces local bearing and bending; a high uniaxial compression value is not a contact allowable. Fiber termination, hoop reinforcement and through-wall splitting require separate treatment in CMCs. Larger contact areas or collars may be necessary but cannot be credited as massless.

The next discriminating evidence is a process-specific drawing and fabrication demonstration of the *combined* geometry, followed by dimensional maps, relevant tube and joint tests, and a defensible statistical allowable. Present evidence supports SiC/SiC as a research alternative with damage tolerance and demonstrated tubular manufacturing; it does not establish a qualified substitute for the thin curved monolithic member.
