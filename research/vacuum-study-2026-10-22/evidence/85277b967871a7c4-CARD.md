The requested physical knockdown is not established. The paper benchmark is not reproduced: converged axisymmetric linear predictions are about 10% high, and the elastic imperfect endpoints exceed the published nonlinear values by more than a factor of two. No calibration factor has been applied to the lead models. All pressures below are numerical results, not hardware measurements.

At the specified pole dimples, the weak-meridian model ends at 78.627 kPa for 021 and 90.722 kPa for 021b, below the 95.461 kPa operating pressure. Strong-meridian variants give higher endpoints, but their result depends materially on the assumed beryllium Poisson ratio and excludes weak-direction nonaxisymmetric modes. None is a certified collapse pressure or a rigorous upper/lower bound.

**The denominator matters.** With `h=tc+(ti+to)/2`, the retained handbook calculation is `p_classical=2 E h (ti+to)/(R² sqrt(1−nu²))`, `p_crimp=2 h² G/(R tc)`, `Vc=p_classical/p_crimp`, and `p_H=Kc*p_classical`, where `Kc=1−Vc/4` for `Vc≤2`, otherwise `1/Vc`. This is the inherited calculation based on [NASA CR-1457](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19700004831/downloads/19700004831.pdf), traced through the sealed 014b/018b/021 inputs. Thus `gamma_crimp=gamma_H*Kc*Vc`. Both lead points are on the moderate-core branch. Their 0.595318/0.468229 design targets use `p_H`; the requested `p/p_crimp` ratios have different targets.

**Numerical and material sensitivities.** Halving the meridional mesh from 512 to 256 elements changes the four specified-defect endpoints by at most 1.925 Pa. Increasing weak-meridian radial resolution from four core/one element per face to six core/two per face leaves both reported endpoints unchanged at native output precision. These checks support discretization repeatability within this model, not its physical validity.

Doubling lead eigenmesh resolution from 512 to 1,024 meridional elements changes the four first eigenpressures by at most 0.0103%. The identical-deck 021b weak-direction static check changes the endpoint by 0 Pa between four and one core.

The one-core weak-direction linear eigenpressures change from 160.1382 to 160.1384 kPa for 021 and from 203.2450 to 203.2509 kPa for 021b when unsourced in-plane core regularization is increased 100-fold. Setting face nu=0.03 lowers the corresponding eigenpressures to 154.2503 and 194.9330 kPa. For the stronger-direction nonlinear path, a declared nu=0.03 sensitivity lowers 021 to 91.468 kPa, below operating pressure. Applying NASA’s SR200 nu12=0.0768 isotropically lowers 021b to 98.347 kPa, only 3.02% above operating pressure. Flat CAX8 eigenpressures are 30.23653, 30.21954 and 30.21717 MPa as meridional resolution increases 96→192→384. This resolves mesh drift but leaves the benchmark discrepancy. The paper uses continuum-shell faces with tie constraints, crushable foam, steel plasticity, contact indentation with stress reset, and ABAQUS Riks continuation. Our conforming solid mesh and elastic laws do not reproduce that formulation; the cause of the linear discrepancy has not been isolated. The full constitutive dataset was not recovered. The foam law and Figure 5 steel-axis ambiguity are open NFI; no graph was rescaled or parameter tuned to force agreement. The paper’s pressure values are simulations; its physical validation is axial compression.

The native finite-strain `*ELASTIC` law is St. Venant–Kirchhoff: PK2 stress depends linearly on Green–Lagrange strain. This differs from Abaqus’s logarithmic-strain/Cauchy elastic convention, and its large-compression response is not a physical foam law. No Ciarlet, hyperfoam or crushable-foam material was specified. Native reported stresses are Cauchy stresses. These conventions are documented in the [CalculiX 2.21 manual, §6.8.1–6.8.2](https://www.dhondt.de/ccx_2.21.pdf). This additional nonlinear limitation does not explain away the unresolved linear benchmark error.
