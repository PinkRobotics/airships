# MAINLINE-057: mass growth

Unit vacuum-mainline057-mass-growth-1020. Order ord-admiral-rr2-mainline057-dispatch-1019. Author: ref-55cff924be25afe6f9dd (claude-opus-5-5, max).

Every value here is QUESTIONABLE or a PROJECTION; nothing is a certification. The owners' bills were read as data; no owner code was run.

## The answer

**Once a sourced concept-stage mass-growth allowance (MGA) is carried, no hull size from R 10 to R 30 floats, for either lead.** That holds:
- in ISA and in optimistic weather;
- for every one of the 16 optimism subsets, with each axis at its endpoint.

| lead | radius | weather | MGA (kg) | payload with MGA (kg) |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|
| P2 | R10 | ISA | 707 | −589 |
| P3 | R10 | ISA | 681 | −650 |
| P2 | R30 | ISA | 18,047 | −6,410 (best case, all axes) |
| P3 | R30 | ISA | 17,988 | −9,092 |

**R 10 is not a credible lead size.** At full optimism, P2 tolerates only 2.8% flat growth on its hardware, and P3 only 0.7%. Both are negative in optimistic weather before any growth. R10 floats only if its masses are flight-proven (the 0% row).

**The design point should move to the largest radius studied, R 30.** Growth tolerance rises with radius (table below). With every axis at its endpoint, R 30 floats in both weathers once the bill is engineering-unit tested (TRL 7, about 5–6% allowance) for P2, and at TRL 7 (ISA) / TRL 8 (optimistic) for P3. No radius floats with the 15–30% that a concept design must carry, so R 30 is the least-bad point, not a closed one.

**What the allowance costs in optimism: more than exists.** No subset of the stated optimism axes absorbs it. Linear extrapolation (PROJECTION) puts the least set's usage at:
- **P2:** 1.78 (R10), 2.13 (R12) and 1.83 (R15), that is 78–113% beyond today's endpoints;
- **P3:** 4.6–30.

## 1. The bills, classified

**The allowance table** is NASA GSFC IDC Table VI-1 (Karpati et al., NTRS 20120013284), adapted from AIAA S-120-2006 Table 1. AIAA S-120A and SAWE RP 7 are paywalled and were not opened (N057-01). The item types used are structure, brackets/clips/hardware and thermal control.

| maturity | TRL row | structure | hardware | thermal control |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| estimated | 0–2 | 25% | 30% | 25% |
| calculated | 3–5 | 15% | 20% | 20% |
| measured | 8 | 3% | 3% | 3% |

The same source adds a 30% system-level margin for Pre-Phase-A concepts on top. It is not applied here, because it would only deepen every deficit (N057-07).

**Rules used:**
- Items sized by analysis but not built are classed **calculated**.
- Items set by rules or catalog areal masses are classed **estimated**.
- Allocations, environmental allowances and lift debits carry no MGA.
- An item that carries its own margin receives only the excess of the table value over that margin, so nothing is counted twice.

## 4. The answer, restated

**Is R 10 still credible once growth is carried?** No. With any sourced concept-stage allowance it sinks: −589 kg (P2) and −650 kg (P3) at full optimism. Its growth tolerance is 2.8% (P2) and 0.7% (P3). It needs flight-proven masses.

**Which size should the design point be?** The largest studied, R 30. It has the most growth tolerance (P2 9.9% ISA / 6.3% optimistic; P3 7.5% / 5.0%) and floats at TRL 7–8 allowances. It does not float at the 15–30% concept-stage allowance. R 20 is the smallest size at which P2 floats in both weathers at TRL 7.

**What does the allowance cost in optimism?** More than the stated ranges hold. The least sets' usage would have to reach 1.78–2.13 (P2) and 4.6–30 (P3); that is a linear extrapolation (N057-05). In mass terms, the MGA exceeds the all-endpoint payload at every radius: by 579 kg at R10 and 6,410 kg at R30 for P2.
