# MAINLINE-047 — P2 dimple tolerance including face yield and core shear

**QUESTIONABLE / PROJECTION. The stated core regularization is not a qualified material law.**

At the held 028b geometry, the assumed **255 and 300 MPa face-yield cases fail even with zero dimple**. With 345 MPa faces and the explicit regularized core below, the governing tested pass–fail bracket is **1.209–1.227 mm at SF 1** and **0.738–0.756 mm at SF 1.05**. Face yield governs these small-dimple boundaries; core shear has now been checked. The lower amplitude passes the model screen and the upper amplitude fails. These are numerical brackets for the tested Gaussian family, not physical uncertainty or fabrication allowables.

The corresponding one-Gaussian RMS estimates about the prescribed sphere are **14.32/8.78 micrometres**. A small full-sphere RMS does not limit other defects or a different spatial spectrum.

## Criterion, geometry and core law

`min(P_endpoint, P_first_face_yield, P_first_core_shear) >= SF × 95.46083791 kPa`. The SF 1.05 target is 100.23387981 kPa. Endpoint means the last converged static pressure, not an experimentally validated collapse pressure. The high-yield target controls follow the identical pre-yield branch; their 10,000 MPa numerical threshold is not a material strength. Actual 345 MPa native runs confirm the governing pass/fail edges.

R_outer = 10,000 mm; each Be face = 0.794624066985892 mm; core = 23.23721790601544 mm. The 037b 200 °F cure / 30.3414:1 scarf correction is retained. Faces use E = 290 GPa, nu = 0.0768, finite-strain isotropic J2 perfect plasticity. The full-sphere CAX8 model uses a stress-free Gaussian inward radial offset of every layer, shared-node bonds and the minimum axisymmetric restraints.

**Final conditional core law:** orthotropic StVK, E_normal = 103.4213594 MPa, transverse G_L/G_W = 103.4213594/46.8843496 MPa, **E_in-plane = 1 MPa and G_in-plane = 0.5 MPa**, zero Poisson coupling. W/L cases interchange meridian and hoop directions. In-plane properties are an explicit regularization sensitivity. No post-failure core plasticity or damage redistribution is credited.

**The inherited E_in-plane = 0.01 MPa law is not accepted for the final tolerance.** CHECK-029 demonstrated loss of ellipticity in that law near 0.088 MPa transverse shear and refined-mesh instability on its 021/021b dimples. It found its 1 MPa sensitivity stable. The present result explicitly uses that stronger regularization; changing an unsourced modulus does not establish the real honeycomb response. [CHECK-029 evidence](002b20b3c78cb099-CHECK.md) is pinned; no checker code was read or run.

Core strengths are from [Hexcel CR III Table II](https://www.hexcel.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/HexWeb_CRIII_DataSheet.pdf), 5056, 3/8-inch cell, 1.0 pcf: W/L **25/45 psi minimum average** and **35/55 psi typical** (0.172369/0.310264 MPa and 0.241317/0.379212 MPa). Both readings are evaluated. These are product-type catalog tests at 0.625-inch core thickness, not installed-hull allowables. The two current transverse shear tractions use a stated quadratic interaction; the native circumferential component is retained.

The initial campaign stored only face stresses and pole displacements, so its fields could not support a core post-check. New native runs save core strain and stress. Current material-frame shear is reconstructed from the full Green-strain tensor and the stated StVK law, then checked by rotation-invariant Cauchy stress quantities.

The maximum reconstructed/native stress-invariant difference is 1.34e-06 MPa. At every saved point/increment on the passing boundary paths, a sufficient local acoustic lower bound remains positive (minimum 0.314142 MPa). This checks local strong ellipticity of the chosen law at those states; it does not prove global or nonaxisymmetric stability. Maximum core compression on these passing paths is 0.066059 MPa, below the catalog 35-psi typical crush strength. The derivation and limitations are in METHOD.md.

The 345 MPa maker value is tensile; 036 found no matched finished-sheet biaxial compressive minimum. All three yield values are assumptions, and an offset yield is idealized as a sharp plastic onset. The thicker-sheet nu value in [NASA CR-194164 Table 2](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19940006718/downloads/19940006718.pdf) does not establish thin-gauge isotropy.

## Conditional benchmark transfer

If endpoint predictions are **10% high**, dividing by **1.10** gives a common SF 1 amplitude bracket **0.521–0.539 mm**, limited here by 401.856 mm/W. Every footprint/orientation is explicitly checked at the common passing amplitude; at least one fails at the upper edge. First face yield and first core shear are included in that minimum. SF 1.05 fails the 1.10 scenario even for the perfect sphere. Applying the 2.3146–2.4589 imperfect-endpoint factors likewise leaves no admissible amplitude even at SF 1.

This is not an empirical calibration of the Be hull. CHECK-029 demonstrates that the predecessor nonlinear gap arose from StVK foam collapse and core discretization; its linear radius/material explanation remains unestablished. The [EUCASS benchmark](https://www.eucass.eu/doi/EUCASS2023-367.pdf) is a different steel/PVC hemisphere with different constitutive and imperfection definitions. The original eight-width calibration calculations and exact linear factors remain in the explicitly historical face-only appendix.
