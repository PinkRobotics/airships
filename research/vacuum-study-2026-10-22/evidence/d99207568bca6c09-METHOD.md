## Why the two radiation terms are different

For two points inside an unobstructed sphere, cos(theta_1)cos(theta_2)/distance²
=1/(4R²). The differential view factor is therefore dA/(4 pi R²).
Irradiation G is spatially constant. Averaging the uniform-emissivity
radiosity equation J=epsilon sigma Ti^4+(1-epsilon)G gives
G=sigma<Ti^4>, hence qi=epsilon sigma(Ti^4-<Ti^4>) and <qi>=0.

That exchange redistributes heat **among inner hull locations**. Core-gap
exchange transports heat **between the two skins of one sandwich panel**.
The solid term uses bulk-metal conductivity times metal volume fraction,
not a measured total panel conductivity. Adding a distinct core-radiation
path does not duplicate the inner-enclosure path in this stated network.
If a measured apparent panel conductivity already contains radiation, adding
the gap term again would double count that part of the panel transport.

For two unobstructed infinite gray plates with emissivity .20 on each side,
q=sigma(To^4-Ti^4)/(1/.20+1/.20-1), so effective emissivity is 1/9.
The assumed .20 is 1.8 times that two-plate value. Honeycomb walls, adhesive
coverage, contact resistance and nonuniform temperatures require their own
model or measurement; 1/9 is a diagnostic sensitivity, not a qualified
replacement for this assembly. The global mean temperature used to linearize
the gap term is another approximation. `GAP-SENSITIVITY.json` preserves the
zero, 1/9 and .20 results.
