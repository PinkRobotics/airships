# MAINLINE-020 — Beryllium reality

ESTIMATE. All sourced and inherited material values are **QUESTIONABLE**. Unverified panel and joining capabilities below are **PROJECTION**. This card supplies sensitivities and open questions, not certification.

The pinned 009c5 lead is a 10 m radius P2 sphere with two 0.75243 mm Be faces and a 20.6423 mm HRH-10 core. Its faces weigh **3,498.47 kg**, not the brief's approximate 2,100 kg. Total mass is 4,644.97 kg, leaving 244.47 kg payload against 4,889.44 kg lift. Its least SF is **0.650412**, a 34.96% capacity shortfall relative to demand, requiring 1.5375 times the calculated capacity to reach SF 1. Own arithmetic reproduced the pinned point and all four selected 014b alternatives. The 2,100 kg figure is retained only as a separate procurement reference.

**1. Supply and price — QUESTIONABLE.** The optimistic supply comparison uses revised 2024 world mine output of 433 tonnes; the conservative comparison uses the 2025 estimate of 430 tonnes. These are historical Be-content totals, not finished-sheet capacity. The brief's 2,100 kg is 0.485–0.488%; the actual faces are 0.808–0.814%. USGS's $1,600/kg is contained Be in copper master alloy, so actual S-200F and SR-200 supplier prices remain **UNKNOWN**. Explicit budget assumptions, not quotes: S-200F $4,800/$9,600 per kg and rolled sheet $9,600/$24,000 per kg, optimistic/conservative. These are owner-selected 3/6 and 6/15 multiples of that imperfect anchor. At assumed 85%/50% fabrication yield, actual-face sheet stock costs $39.51m/$167.93m; the 2,100 kg reference costs $23.72m/$100.80m. Forming, assembly, controls and scrap credit are excluded. Obtain a grade-specific quote before using these figures as a budget. [USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-beryllium.pdf)

**2. Sheet, shape and seams — source values QUESTIONABLE; proposed panels PROJECTION.** Current SR-200 data lists 609.6 × 1,828.8 mm maximum stock for thicknesses at least 0.059 inch, while thinner 0.020–0.058 inch stock has unspecified smaller dimensions. Largest purchasable 0.75 mm sheet is therefore **UNKNOWN**. Optimistic planning transfers that large size to thin sheet; conservative planning assumes 304.8 × 609.6 mm. They imply at least 1,128/6,764 panels per skin and about 2,256/13,528 shared edge joints per skin: 2.749/6.184 km of coincident hull seams, doubled for individual skin seams. These are area approximations, not layouts. Hot forming is a documented route, but the 10 m double curvature needs tooling and post-form inspection. Flat-panel corner sag is 46.56/5.81 mm. Optimistic Type-3 flatness is 0.762 mm per foot; conservative Type-1 is 2% of span, potentially measured restrained. Neither is an installed-shell imperfection guarantee. Thickness tolerance near this gauge is ±0.1016 mm: a local low gauge screens at SF 0.52323; a uniformly high gauge adds 472.40 kg of faces and removes the float margin. **3. Joining — QUESTIONABLE process evidence; PROJECTION hull joints.** Adhesive bonding and brazing are established Be processes. Historical thick Be/steel double-shear coupons reached 85–112.5% tensile efficiency, which does not establish this thin shell's compression, peel or leak capacity. Assume 90%/60% net-section retention for screening; the resulting least SF is 0.65041/0.53535. Own 60/120 mm Be splice-doubler models weigh 0.218/0.483 kg per coincident hull-seam metre, counting both skins, bond film, core-gap paste and sealant. Full-area face/core film at 0.030/0.060 lb/ft² adds 368.13/736.25 kg. Large-panel optimistic joining totals 968.20 kg; small-panel conservative joining totals 3,722.00 kg. Replacing the existing 216.43 kg allowance leaves payload **−507.30/−3,261.10 kg**. Brazed filler-only scenarios give 0.0054/0.020 kg/m, but exclude structural lands, repairs and proven seam geometry; they are not complete joint masses. Braze the metal subassembly before incorporating organic core and adhesive. [Materion joining guide, pp. 13–14](https://hkvxni.files.cmp.optimizely.com/download/dafce45ca04211ee97ee126db737ac74), [NASA joint tests, pp. 17–22](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19690025590/downloads/19690025590.pdf), [3M film weights](https://multimedia.3m.com/mws/media/282041O/3m-scotch-weld-structural-adhesive-film-af-163-2-af-163-3.pdf?fn=AF163-2.pdf)

**4. Mechanical properties and daily cycles — QUESTIONABLE.** Optimistic inherited compression is 283 MPa; the conservative screen uses 241 MPa, a bulk S-200F tensile minimum, explicitly **not** a compression guarantee. Current E is 290 GPa versus inherited 303 GPa; this alone reduces the wrinkle-controlled least SF to 0.63631. Bulk S-200F minimum elongation is 2%; rolled SR-200 gives 10% over a different gauge length. Cross-rolling balances in-plane behavior but does not make through-thickness and post-form performance interchangeable. Optimistic directional retention is 1.0; assumed conservative E/strength retentions of 0.8/0.7 screen at SF 0.53188. They are sensitivity factors, not measured anisotropy. Reapplying 016's shear-lag equation to Be/HRH-10 with assumed mismatch 5/20 ppm/K and temperature change 30/60 K gives 0.0577/0.4616 MPa peak shear. Actual effective CTE and joint fatigue life remain unknown. Smooth bulk Be's 262–266 MPa, 10⁷-cycle, zero-mean benchmark cannot qualify the assumed 10,958 daily cycles over 30 years under the hull's 317 MPa compressive demand. [Bulk properties](https://hkvxni.files.cmp.optimizely.com/download/c2eca2d0f75e11ee9aa0f6c265376dea), [rolled properties](https://hkvxni.files.cmp.optimizely.com/download/c3370ecef75e11eeb4768ed6ed17dca6), [IAEA beryllium data](https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/Pub23vol5_web.pdf)

**5. Toxicity as process and cost — QUESTIONABLE.** Airborne Be exposure can cause sensitization and chronic beryllium disease. Use an equipped specialist process with enclosed/local exhaust or wet machining, appropriate filtration, exposure monitoring, hygiene, waste controls and applicable medical surveillance. The US industrial reference limits are 0.2 µg/m³ over eight hours and 2.0 µg/m³ over fifteen minutes; site jurisdiction must be established. Optimistic planning assumes an existing controlled contractor and adds 0.10F to otherwise quoted conversion cost F. Conservative planning adds 0.30F plus separately quoted facility capital/commissioning C. These are owner cost allowances, not regulatory percentages; F and C remain unpriced. This is a procurement/process row, not a reason to stop the material study. [OSHA beryllium standard](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1024)

**6. Best beryllium-free fallback — QUESTIONABLE.** K13D2U/epoxy is the best real-material candidate in the pinned 014b comparison. At its separately sized zero-payload design, SF 0.65193 gives up 0.03341, or 4.87%, against Be's comparable 0.68534. M55J gives 0.59349, losing 13.40%; AlBeMet gives 0.55399, losing 19.16%, and contains **62 wt% Be**. At the exact 009c5 geometry, K13D2U gives 0.54147, losing 16.75%, with 443.03 kg payload under the inherited joint formula. Resizing its published design to the same 5% payload budget gives 0.61865, without reoptimization. Thus the optimistic fallback comparison is 0.61865 at equal mass budget; the conservative direct substitution is 0.54147 at equal geometry. Its quasi-isotropic laminate properties and real joints still need grounding. Pinned 014b [source link unavailable in this public projection], [AlBeMet composition](https://hkvxni.files.cmp.optimizely.com/download/d896d6d2a04211ee94aa3aa4a5458726)

Ranked by effect on the lead: installed bonds/seams first; gauge, panel size and formed geometry second; material orientation and post-form compression third; thermal fatigue fourth; fallback laminate feasibility fifth; supply, price and controlled-process economics sixth. **Ground two things first:** a weighed, curved, sealed two-skin joint/core panel with measured load transfer; and a supplier-confirmed thin-sheet specification with a gauge map and post-form compression/biaxial evidence.
