The OSHA limits (0.2 and 2.0 µg/m³) and AlBeMet's 62 wt% beryllium have no local copy, as the card says. Both agree with what I know of those sources, but I could not hash them.

**Objections.** Each is a row in NFI.json, and none stops anything.

1. NFI-020C-GRADE-BASIS, a proposed fix. Sections 1 and 2 buy SR-200 sheet, but sections 3 and 4 screen strength with S-200F block values. The S-200F datasheet lists S-200F in rod and bar, and points to SR-200 sheet and PS-200 foil for beryllium under 6.3 mm. SR-200's minimum tensile yield is 345 MPa (S03, and guide page 8), above the card's "optimistic" 283 MPa. I transferred it to compression the way the card transfers 241 MPa. Face compression at the lead is then 1.0877, against the card's 0.8923 and 0.7598. Two screens change:
   - at η = 0.6 the least SF stays 0.65041, not 0.53535;
   - the 0.8E/0.7σ screen gives 0.56913 (wrinkling), not 0.53188.

   The fix is to add an SR-200 row and keep 241 MPa as an explicitly block-grade lower screen. Whether SR-200 reaches 345 MPa in in-plane compression stays open under the owner's NFI-020-GRADE-ORIENTATION-COMPRESSION.
2. NFI-020C-FALLBACK-E-BASIS, a proposed fix. Section 4 takes 290 GPa as beryllium's current modulus, but section 6 compares K13D2U with beryllium at 303 GPa. At 290 GPa, beryllium's published 014b design drops to 0.67059, governed by wrinkling. The SF given up by choosing K13D2U is then smaller:

   | Basis | Card, at 303 GPa | At 290 GPa |
   |---|---|---|
   | Published designs | 4.87% | 2.78% |
   | 009c5 geometry | 16.75% | 14.90% |
   | 5% budget | 4.88% (not printed) | 2.77% |

   The fix is to state section 6 at both moduli. The ranking does not change.
3. NFI-020C-DOUBLER-BERYLLIUM. Section 3's splice doublers are full-gauge beryllium: 459.1 kg optimistic and 2,066.0 kg conservative. Section 1 counts only the faces. With the doublers, beryllium is 0.914% and 1.294% of mine output. Sheet stock then costs $44.70m and $267.10m, which is +13% and +59%.

**Seen, not raised.**
- Dimpling is screened uniaxially, as 014b does. The biaxial straight-line rule halves it to 26.3, which still does not bind.
- The 10,958 cycles is 30 × 365.25 = 10,957.5, rounded up.
- SR-200's datasheet converts 0.059 in to "0.5 mm"; it is 1.50 mm. The owner already notes this.
- The brief's 2,100 kg is not the lead's face mass, and the card's correction to 3,498.47 kg stands.
