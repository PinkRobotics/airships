## The answer

1. **A route can be laid out, but only as a chain of smaller or other-material precedents.**
   - Each step has been done somewhere: a 10 m aluminium sandwich dome cured in an autoclave (S-II), a Be honeycomb panel creep-formed to 0.003 in (cylindrical, 1 x 2 ft), reflector panels to 5-25 µm, spheres to ±6-20 mm, and vacuum-stress shearography.
   - None has been done in Be sandwich at hull scale.
   - Two steps have no precedent at all: about 5.7 km of in-place, room-temperature 30:1-class scarf joints in 0.8 mm Be, and a 20 m bonded sandwich shell cured without an autoclave or oven that can hold it.
2. **The shape the P2 point needs is not tight by aerospace standards.**
   - At 028b's point (t_eq 41.6 mm, R/t 240), gamma 0.564 allows one dimple of 9.3-16.1 mm over about 0.36 m (015b's map). That is a single-dimple RMS of 0.12-0.20 mm.
   - Q 0.435 allows face waviness of 0.075-0.129 mm (032's map).
3. **What precedents hold.**
   - Dome-class builds hold dimples of about 1-5 mm. The map turns that into gamma 0.69-0.90.
   - Reflector-class builds (about 0.15 mm) map to about 0.95.
   - Reflector-panel faces (10-24 µm RMS) map to Q 0.59-0.75.
   - Ordinary vendor honeycomb (0.2 mm RMS) maps to Q 0.19-0.28, which fails 0.435.
4. **But the map is not the record.**
   - S-II's bulkheads were built with a 0.254 mm core-to-face fit and inflation-faired skins, and its sandwich hemispheres measured about 0.21-0.22 (035, CHECK-035).
   - Shape control alone did not deliver the map's knockdown.
   - **The route therefore supports gamma of about 0.2 on the record.** 0.564 needs a built shell with measured shape and measured knockdown (038 T7).
   - Q 0.435 needs reflector-grade face finish, which nobody has measured on Be.
5. **The hardest step is the seams.** That means 2,842 m of coincident seam, each with two 30.34:1 scarfs (037b): in place, at room temperature, inner skin from inside, with no oven, no seam NDE method, and Be cutting kept out of the assembly hall.
6. **The first build-side test is F1, the full-blank forming-and-seam article.** It should come after 038's T1 (sheet specification) and absorb T2 (the 600 mm curved scarf panel).

## The P2 point and the shape it needs

- **The point.** 028b's nominal AF/scarf point:
  - Faces: 0.794624 mm SR-200.
  - Core: 23.2372 mm CR-III 5056 3/8-1.0.
  - Blanks and seams: 0.6096 x 1.8288 m blanks, 30:1 scarfs with a 23.84 mm overlap, 2,841.5 m of coincident seam.
  - Joining: 215.742 kg.
  - Needs: gamma 0.564164 and Q 0.434628.
- **Gamma map.** This is 015b's live map, re-implemented in build042.py and reproducing 015b's rows to the printed digits: the Be point at kd 0.9, 021 and 021b.
  - Baizhikova Eq. 3: kd = 0.45 + 0.51 exp(-3.88 d/t).
  - Lee Eq. 23: kd = 0.068 + 0.25/(0.28 + d/t).
  - Both run on t_eq = √3 (tc + tf) = 41.62 mm, with ν 0.33 and λ_I = 1.
  - At gamma 0.564164: Eq. 3 gives 16.06 mm (d/t 0.386, inside its fit) and Lee gives 9.32 mm (d/t 0.224, inside its FEM range). The dimple width R·β0 is 0.357 m, and the single-dimple RMS is 0.203 / 0.118 mm.
- **Q map.** This is 032's CR-1457 Eq. 2.2-12: Q = 0.82/(1 + 0.64K), K = d·Ec/(tc·Fc). It reproduces 032's rows at their 20.21 and 30.12 mm cores.
  - Q 0.434628 allows 0.1288 mm (Fc 60 psi typical) or 0.0751 mm (35 psi minimum).
  - CHECK-032 puts the wrinkle period near 37-40 mm.

## The route (step 2)

1. **Sheet.** SR-200 at 0.795 mm is in the 0.020-0.058 in class, sold "at narrower widths and shorter lengths" than 24 x 72 in (DS-HPP-613, V).
   - So 028b's 0.61 x 1.83 m blank is itself a projection. Smaller sheets multiply blanks and seams (020: up to 6,764 per skin).
   - At 028b's size there are 1,189 blanks per skin.
   - The gauge tolerance is ±0.102 mm (±12.8%). A thin patch under load sinks about 1.2 mm at blank scale.
   - Chemical milling can trim gauge (about 0.001 in per 3 min, sub-worker), but one maker declined to etch large thin sheets.
   - Map the gauge of every sheet (038 T1).
2. **Forming the faces.** A flat blank needs in-plane strain to reach R 10 m: about 4.6e-4 across 0.61 m (67 MPa) and about 1.2e-3 over the half-diagonal disc. The second figure is at Be's 1.19e-3 elastic limit.
   - **Hot creep forming** is the documented route: about 1250-1350 °F, ceramic dies, vacuum or argon.
     - Solar held 0.003 in on a cylindrical 1 x 2 ft panel (V).
     - Lockheed's 39 in skins warped from thermal gradients (sub-worker).
     - No doubly curved blank of this size has a measured contour.
   - **Cold conforming** during bonding would leave residual stress and springback. The CSO panels showed the same effect at 150 µm.
3. **Core.** CR-III 5056 at 23.2 mm, perforated so it vents inward (034: otherwise 43.4 kg of trapped air), and bent to radius as fairing cores are.
   - Fit it to each face's measured contour as S-II did: machined to match within 0.010 in.
4. **Face-core bond and its cure.** AF 163-2U per 028.
   - **Cure temperature.** 250 °F leaves the bond 109 K above service, with a Be/Al-core mismatch strain of 1.35e-3. 3M's 200 °F, 6 h cure (V) leaves 82 K and 1.00e-3.
   - **037b's verdict** (rpt-ref-d6eac96dde8db1968e86, filed 03:24; its checker is running):
     - With the faces carrying the in-plane load, the continuous core bond survives a hot cure.
     - The cost lands in the faces instead: a bond stress-free above 54 °C passes balanced compression through the 30:1 scarf. At 250 °F that is 4.4 MPa, and 028b's usage rises from 0.972 to 1.052.
     - Its lightest fix is AF 163-2U's own 200 °F cure plus a 30.34:1 scarf: +0.12 kg, usage 0.972.
     - **This route adopts that fix.** Face-core bonds cure at 200 °F at panel level; seams cure at room temperature.
   - **Room-temperature paste.** EA 9394 or EA 9309.3NA (29-33 MPa lap shear on Al, sub-worker) cost 25.1 kg per extra 10 g/m² on both bond lines. 028b closes with 2 kg to spare.
   - **Cure envelope.** The sphere's 4,189 m³ is 1.8 times the largest autoclave's whole volume (2,321 m³, ASC). Its 20 m exceeds the largest diameter found (9.14 m, Spirit).
     - S-II's 10 m dome needed a "gargantuan autoclave".
     - NASA cures 10 m barrels out of autoclave.
     - So cure the face-core bonds at panel level, in an envelope of about 0.7 x 1.9 m, which any autoclave or oven holds.
5. **Seams.** 2,841.5 m of coincident seam: two 30.34:1 scarfs (037b's rule; 24.1 mm overlap, against 028b's 23.8 mm at 30:1), a core splice and a seal.
   - **They must cure in place at room temperature.** A seam strip heated 100 K above the surrounding shell carries about 354 MPa, above Be's 345 MPa. Heat blankets (12-16 °C spread at 250 °F, sub-worker) are for repairs.
   - **Cut the Be before assembly.** Scarfing Be makes dust, which falls under OSHA 1910.1024 (PEL 0.2 µg/m³). Every scarf is therefore cut at the supplier, as JPL did; the assembly hall only bonds.
   - **The inner-skin seams** are made from inside, and the last ones through a manhole.
6. **Holding the shape while the shell closes.** There are two ways.
   - **(a) Panelised.** Cured sandwich panels go on a 20 m jig.
     - Supports ≤ 2 m apart keep self-weight sag to 0.11 mm; at 5 m it is 4.2 mm.
     - A laser tracker sets them (0.19 mm MPE at 20 m, V).
     - Large spheres built this way held ±6 mm (SNO) to under 2 cm (Moss).
   - **(b) S-II inflation, my proposal (PROJECTION).**
     - Build the inner Be skin first as a closed sphere on a low-pressure bladder.
     - Inflate it as S-II inflated its skins (1/2 psi). That puts 21.7 MPa of tension in the 0.8 mm Be, about 180 times the skin's bending term at the 0.36 m dimple scale.
     - Map it, fit the core to the map, and bond the core and outer skin from outside.
7. **Evacuation (034).**
   - Vent the core inward and evacuate on the ground. 034 selects sealed service at 30-50 kg onboard.
   - **The first evacuation is the proof.** At sea level it loads the hull to 1.061 of its 500 m design pressure (038). A buckle there ends the hull.

- **Small dimples fall outside the fits.** Below d/t 0.07, Eq. 3 is outside its fit. Below 0.15 (FEM) or 0.2 (ODE), Lee is outside its range.
- **The waviness figures are not band-limited.** They are whole-panel RMS figures, not the wrinkle-band component (032b, CHECK-032).
- **Reading.** By the map, every plausible build class clears 0.564 easily, yet the only measured large sandwich domes sit at 0.21-0.22. The map leaves out four things:
  - residual stress from forming and cure;
  - the ±12.8% gauge;
  - seam stiffness steps;
  - core shear at Vc 0.95, and bond flaws.
- **So the build does not certify gamma by holding shape.** A built shell must be measured, in shape and in knockdown, before anything above about 0.2 is claimed.

## The answer (step 5)

- **Is there a credible route?** Not yet credible; plausible in parts.
  - Each step has a precedent somewhere, but none in Be sandwich at this scale.
  - The route needs two firsts:
    - kilometre-scale room-temperature Be scarf seams made in place;
    - a 20 m bonded sandwich shell with no cure envelope.
  - The face-core cure is settled on paper by 037b: 200 °F at panel level, with a 30.34:1 scarf (+0.12 kg).
- **What gamma and Q does it support?**
  - By the map, 0.69-0.95. On the record, about 0.2.
  - Q 0.435 needs waviness of 0.075-0.129 mm at the wrinkle wavelength. Reflector-grade practice suggests about 0.6 is reachable (PROJECTION). Ordinary sandwich practice gives about 0.2-0.3.
- **What is the hardest step?** The seams (route step 5). Forming doubly curved blanks at full size without warping them (step 2) is second.
- **What is the first build-side test?** F1:
  - two full-size SR-200 blank pairs, hot-formed to R 10 m, with gauge and contour maps before and after forming;
  - bonded at panel level to vented CR-III core;
  - joined by one in-place room-temperature scarf seam on a curved fixture;
  - measured for dimples at the 0.36 m scale (photogrammetry) and waviness in the 20-60 mm band (profiler);
  - the seam inspected by shearography and then sectioned.
- **F1 against 038's list.**
  - 038's T2 (a 600 mm curved scarf panel) is the nearest test.
  - F1 adds the full blank size, where forming strain reaches Be's elastic limit and large skins warp. It also adds the in-place room-temperature seam, and shape metrics tied to gamma and Q.
  - It follows T1, can supply T2 and T3's coupons, and stays short of T7, which is still the only test that can ground gamma.
  - Cost is dominated by Be stock: four blanks of about 1.65 kg each, at 038's USD 10-50k/kg planning range (PROJECTION).
